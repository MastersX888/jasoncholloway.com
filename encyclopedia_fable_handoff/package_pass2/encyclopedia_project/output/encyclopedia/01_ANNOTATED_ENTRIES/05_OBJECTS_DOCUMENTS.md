## THE ALDRIC CODEX (1267 · THE 712 FIGURES)

**In the novels.** Brother Aldric's seven-year transcription, completed in Bohemia in **1267** (`canon/CANON.md` §3): **seven hundred and twelve** proportional figures on calf vellum "scraped so thin that candlelight passed through it like stained glass," in ink "that had cost the monastery three months' tithes" — lamp black and iron gall, thickened with gum arabic. Forty calves; each blessed. The final figure: "a circle inscribed within a circle, bisected by lines that met at angles his mathematics could not name," copied from an original "written by hands not entirely human" (*Omnibus*, prologue, pp. 9–10). The codex is the deep ancestor of everything the Strahov crypt holds.

**In the world.** Period-accurate manuscript craft. Real parallel: **Codex Gigas** (13th-c. Bohemian giant manuscript) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Codex_Gigas). Undecipherable-figures tradition: Voynich Manuscript, Codex Seraphinianus (`01_RESEARCH_CATALOG.md`).

**Cross-references:** Aldric, Brother · Ars Notoria · Cymatics & the Notae · 1267 · 712.

## THE ARS NOTORIA

**In the novels.** The grimoire Nadia opens on Blake's kitchen floor — reframed from "superstitious nonsense" to "cognitive technology": the notae as interfaces (*Omnibus*, Vol. I ch. 6). With "the Testament," it supplies the physiological site-map Blake verifies at Chartres: "manuscripts are theory. Stone is evidence" (*Omnibus*, Vol. II ch. 8, "The Stone Voices").

**In the world.** *Ars Notoria* is a real medieval text — the "Notory Art" attributed to Solomon, a manual of accelerated learning through prayers, orations, and the contemplation of geometric *notae*; Robert Turner's 17th-century English translation is the package's source text (`Ars Notoria - The Notory Art of Solomon`, `01_RESEARCH_CATALOG.md`), with the wider Solomonic corpus alongside it (`Esoterica/Lesser_Key_of_Solomon_Goetia.txt`, `Sworn_Book_of_Honorius.txt`). Medieval universities really did condemn it as illicit shortcut-learning. Its efficacy is the fiction.

**Cross-references:** Cymatics & the Notae · The Aldric Codex · Strahov Monastery & Crypt.

## THE MOLESKINE NOTEBOOKS

**In the novels.** The Masters family's medium of record. William leaves **seven** leather notebooks in the closet safe — "Seven notebooks. Thirty years" (*Omnibus*, Vol. I); Blake continues the **Moleskine** series. The epigraph is signed "Blake Masters, Moleskine IX"; Vol. I's manuscript is "set down in the seventh Moleskine" (*Omnibus*, Vol. I epilogue). By trilogy close Blake has filled nine in Iceland and completes a **tenth**; nine are returned packed (*Omnibus*, Vol. III).

**In the world.** The fragmented-notebook strategy parallels Billings-record information control (`research_synthesis/ROGER_BILLINGS_MASTERS_X_ANALYSIS.md`). The notebook-as-exegesis form is PKD's: *Exegesis* (`research_synthesis/PKD_EXEGESIS_MASTERS_REFERENCE.md`). **Do not cite "seventeen" for William** — that figure does not appear in v8 (see `output/VERIFICATION_LOG.md`).

**Cross-references:** Masters, William · Masters, Blake · The Motif Watchers · 111.2 Hz.

## THE 1967 BREITLING NAVITIMER

**In the novels.** Three generations on one wrist. "A **1967** Breitling Navitimer, the kind pilots wore when pilots still navigated by the stars" (year canon-locked, `canon/CANON.md` §3). William bought it the year he left Northrop; James wore it until September 2010; Blake adjusts it at the Miller Nichols corner table in chapter one, reading *Foucault's Pendulum* — "Fourth time through" (*Omnibus*, Vol. I ch. 1). In Iceland it closes the first volume's final paragraph: "the Moleskine, the Breitling, the stars over the lava field." Nadia's verdict: "The watch is good."

**In the world.** The Navitimer is the real 1952-designed pilot's chronograph. Blake reads *Foucault's Pendulum* at the Miller Nichols table — literary genome per `research_synthesis/UMBERTO_ECO_MASTERS_REFERENCE.md`.

**Cross-references:** Masters, William · Masters, James · Masters, Blake · 1967.

## THE MOTIF WATCHERS

**In the novels.** James Andrew Masters' manuscript — "Two hundred forty pages. The last thing James Andrew Masters had written," held in the Keepers' archive fourteen years before Blake reads it: "His father's voice rose from rough prose. An engineer's sentences, direct and functional. Three generations discovering geometr[y]…" (*Omnibus*, Vol. II). The middle generation's testimony, bridging William's notebooks and Andrew Chen's Distribution File — private record, institutional record, public record.

## THE DISTRIBUTION FILE

**In the novels.** The trilogy's terminal artifact: **247 pages**, compiled by Andrew Chen for the William Masters Foundation in seventy-two hours and released **CC0 public domain** at midnight from the Reykjanes control module. Week-one telemetry from its own front matter: 1,204,881 downloads; 47 countries; 5 community translations; 3 independent replications (Kyoto, Buenos Aires, Lagos). "The click of the key that released this file measured approximately 3,200 Hz and lasted four milliseconds" (*DF*, pp. i–ii). Five parts, each keyed to trilogy chapters (*DF*, contents page): I. Preparation Protocols (pp. 1–58) · II. Chamber Specifications (59–112) · III. Harmonic Frequency Derivations (113–178) · IV. Facilitator Training Manual (179–220) · Appendix. Acoustic Research (221–247). Chen's design brief: "deliberately dry, deliberately reproducible… the surest way to end the secrecy was to make the work boring" (*DF*, p. iii).

**In the world.** The document exists — Seventh City Press produced it as a real 247-page in-universe artifact (`sources/distribution_file_fulltext.txt`; page count confirmed in `universe_memory/04_STORY_CANON_DIGEST.md`). Its form belongs to the *S.*/*House of Leaves* artifact-book tradition the publication plan cites as comps (`encyclopedia_project/01_PUBLICATION_PLAN.md` §2–3). Its content synthesizes the real research corpus annotated throughout this encyclopedia.

**Cross-references:** Chen, Andrew · The Masters Foundation · Fifty-Two-Week Protocol · 02_DISTRIBUTION_FILE_INTEGRATION.md · 247.

## THE SPECCHI MIRRORS

**In the novels.** The chamber's optical heart. The Specchi family of Murano — "active from approximately 1340 to 1720. Three hundred and eighty years of continuous mirror production… not the most commercially successful… but the most specialized" — hand-delivered their mirrors (Prague, Paris, Barcelona) rather than shipping through intermediaries; an **1843** commission went to "a Jesuit mission… in the western land," twenty-seven years before a date the Kansas City thread turns on (*Omnibus*, Vol. II; Blake's Venice trip). The Foundation's Moreau chamber runs "six parabolic surfaces, each ground to the Specchi specification by the Venetian workshop that Eva Černá had contra[cted]" (*Omnibus*, Vol. III).

**In the world.** Murano mirror history is documented; Specchi family is fiction. "1843… commission from a Jesuit mission" is in-novel (*Omnibus*, Vol. III). Missouri Jesuit chronology: `research_synthesis/JESUIT_BOOKS_USAGE_GUIDE.md` `[AUTHOR VERIFY — specific 1843 mission ID if encyclopedia names one]`.

## THE STRAHOV CYMATICS DISCS

**In the novels.** The crypt's physical proof-objects — frequency patterns fixed in material form, photographed by Blake in Prague. The second disc becomes the trilogy's cleanest experiment when Yuki Tanaka, blind to the source, reproduces its pattern from a seven-minute single-frequency exposure: "The drawings were identical" (*Omnibus*, Vol. III). The discs are the hinge between manuscript (Aldric's figures) and machine (the chamber).

**In the world.** Frequency-specific standing-wave patterns on plates and membranes are real and reproducible — Chladni figures and Hans Jenny's tonoscope corpus, including his finding that near-identical frequencies (his 800 cps vs. 865 cps example) yield categorically different forms: `research_synthesis/CYMATICS_RESEARCH_SYNTHESIS.md`, Part I.2. Medieval artisans fixing such patterns deliberately is fiction; the physics that would have let them see the patterns is not.

**Cross-references:** Cymatics & the Notae · Tanaka, Yuki · Strahov Monastery & Crypt · The Aldric Codex.
