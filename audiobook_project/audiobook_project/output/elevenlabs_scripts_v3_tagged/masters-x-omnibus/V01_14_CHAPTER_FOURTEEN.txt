Chapter Fourteen. Nadia's Confession.

One eleven point two hertz. Prague.

[short pause]

Blake couldn't sleep.

He stood at the window. Prague's angular language pressing through the walls. The courtyard below was a perfect geometric enclosure. Cobblestones laid in a radial pattern, the same proportional system he'd found in the caves.

He opened the adjoining door. Nadia was sitting in the window seat, knees drawn up, looking out at the courtyard. She'd changed into a sweater and jeans. Her hair was down. It changed the shape of her face. Made her look younger. Less composed.

"Can't sleep either," she said without turning.

"Prague won't stop talking."

"It never does. Sit."

He sat on the other end of the window seat. Their feet almost touched. The courtyard below was empty. Medieval stone, a single streetlamp, pigeons roosting on ledges that had held pigeons for four hundred years.

"I want to tell you something," Nadia said. "Before tomorrow. Before the chamber."

"Okay."

"About me. About who I was before any of this."

Blake waited.

"I was born in Lviv. 1994. My mother was a cleaning woman. She died when I was six. House fire." A pause. "The Keepers found me afterward. They'd been tracking my mother's family for generations. Bloodlines that show potential."

"Your mother had the gift?"

"She had something. Not as strong as yours. More like intuition. She could tell when something was wrong in a building just by walking through it." Nadia's voice was quiet, factual. "The nuns at the orphanage in Prague were Keepers. They raised me. Taught me languages. By twelve, I could translate thirteenth-century Latin faster than most graduate students."

"They were training you to be a Keeper."

"They were training me to be useful to the Keepers. There's a difference." She turned from the window. Something rawer in her eyes than Blake had seen. "I took my vows at twenty-one. Not because I believed in God. Because the order was the only family I'd ever had."

"And the ring?"

"It means I belong to something. Not God. The tradition. Seven hundred years of women passing knowledge to other women." She turned the ring on her finger. "When I took it off before SubTropolis. That was the first time in ten years."

"Why did you take it off?"

"Because I didn't want anything between my hand and the stone. Or between my hand and yours."

The courtyard was silent. A church bell counted midnight.

"The candidates," Blake said. "How many others?"

"Seven, in my time. A mathematician from Cairo. A painter from São Paulo. A monk from Kyoto. Each one had a piece of the gift. None had all of it."

"And you guided all of them."

"I guided all of them. Taught them the figures. Walked them to the threshold." Her voice dropped. "And each time, I went back to my room and wrote reports and wondered what it felt like. To stand at that door and know you could walk through it."

"It's not fair," Blake said.

"No."

"That you do all the work and someone else gets to see what's inside."

"That's what Keepers do. We keep."

"I'm not going to be another report," Blake said.

"I know."

"I mean it. Whatever happens tomorrow. I'm not going to become a case study. Blake who came back. Every time. Because someone was waiting."

He moved closer. Their knees touched.

"I need to tell you the rest," she said. "About why I really brought you here."

"I know why. The Keepers identified -"

"Not the Keepers' reason. Mine." She took a breath. "Somewhere between the kitchen floor and the vault, somewhere between your ridiculous Thai food and the hand-holding in the dark, I stopped being a Keeper who happened to care about you and became a person who happened to be a Keeper."

"Nadia -"

"Let me finish." She set both hands flat on the window seat. Open. Unguarded. "Tomorrow you're going into a chamber that has broken everyone who entered it alone. And this time, if you can't find the circuit breaker. I won't write a report. I will stay in that chamber until you come back or until they drag me out. Because I'm not your Keeper anymore, Blake."

"What are you?"

She looked at him. The window light. The Prague courtyard. The midnight bell still resonating.

"I'm the person who loves you. And I'm terrified of tomorrow."

Blake felt the ripples spread. He'd known. Since the vault. Since the hand in the dark. But hearing it, in her voice, in Prague, was different from knowing it.

"I'm terrified too," he said.

"Good. Terrified is honest."

"Not just the chamber. Us. If you go into that chamber and come out different, we lose this. We lose the ability to sit on a window seat and be frightened together. That's what the gift takes. Not your life. Your capacity for ordinary human moments."

Blake took her hand. Fingers interlacing. The calluses. And in radiance now. In a moment they'd both chosen.

"Then I'll protect the ordinary moments," he said. "The coffee at 3:47. The Thai food on the kitchen floor. The haircut."

Nadia leaned forward and pressed her forehead against his. Eyes closed. Breathing together. Not a kiss. Something more deliberate. A joining of the part of the body that contained everything, perception, thought, fear, love.

They stayed like that for a long time. Prague humming its ancient language through walls that had held this same kind of moment for six hundred years.

"Come back," she whispered.

"I will."

"Promise."

"I promise."

She pulled back. Touched his jaw. Then she stood.

"Dawn," she said. "Strahov. Four hours."

She walked to the adjoining door. Stopped. Looked back.

"Your grandfather was right about one thing," she said. "The chamber shows you everything. But only love lets you survive it." She opened the door. "I'll be right here. Through this wall. All night."

She closed the door. Blake sat on the window seat, feeling her resonance through stone.

Four hours until dawn. Four hours until the threshold William couldn't cross.

But William had been alone. And Blake wasn't.

Blake lay down. Reached her vibration. Slept.
