## Description (≤ 350 words — trim to taste)

The Masters X Trilogy is fiction. The research behind it is real. This encyclopedia is the
seam between the two — and it never lets you lose sight of which side of the line you are
standing on.

Sixty-nine annotated entries and counting — persons, places, the frequency system, factions,
objects, texts, and the hidden architectures of the Bible-cryptography cluster — each written
in two registers. IN THE NOVELS states what the trilogy asserts, cited to the omnibus text
and to the Distribution File. IN THE WORLD states what the documented record actually shows:
the cymatics experiments, the CIA's Gateway translation, the limestone under Kansas City,
the medieval manuscripts, the genealogies. Where fiction and record disagree, both are
printed. Where a claim could not be sourced, the flag is printed too.

At the book's center, reproduced whole: the Distribution File — the 247-page in-universe
research document at the heart of the trilogy's third volume, reset page-for-page so its
internal pagination is stable in every edition, with a marginal rule on every page marking
it as the artifact it is. Around it: research essays on the facts behind the fiction, the
frequency tables under honest headnotes, an annotated bibliography of the research library,
and a full index.

In the tradition of Eco's annotated apparatus and the scholarship on Philip K. Dick's
Exegesis, this is a reference volume for readers who want to know exactly where the pattern
ends and the world begins.
