# The Grimoire That Was Actually a Study Aid

*The Facts Behind the Fiction, No. 2 · Jason Carroll Holloway · Seventh City Press*

In *The Inheritance of Frequency*, Nadia sits on a kitchen floor with a facsimile page between her hands and says the line the trilogy pivots on: "The Ars Notoria isn't magic. It's technology. Cognitive technology."

Readers assume I invented the manuscript. I did not. The Ars Notoria is real, catalogued, requestable at the British Library. What I invented is narrower, and stranger for its narrowness.

## The manuscript on the record

The Ars Notoria, or the Notory Art, belongs to the Solomonic tradition: medieval texts attributed, nominally, to King Solomon. The earliest surviving manuscripts date to the mid-thirteenth century. Institutional copies survive across Europe, including British Library MS Sloane 1712 and holdings at the Bibliothèque nationale de France. Robert Turner translated it into English in 1657. Modern scholarly editions exist, and Claire Fanger and Julien Véronèse have spent careers on it.

What the text promises is the rapid acquisition of the seven liberal arts, meaning grammar, rhetoric, logic, arithmetic, geometry, music, and astronomy, through structured contemplation of geometric figures called *notae* combined with specific prayers on a prescribed schedule. Not wealth, not love, not power over demons. Knowledge, memory, and eloquence on an accelerated timetable. A medieval manual for learning faster.

The notae are dense, elaborate diagrams, geometric structures carrying prayers in Latin, Hebrew, Greek, and occasionally scripts nobody has identified. They are meant to be gazed at in sequence while the orations are recited. As objects they are unlike anything else in medieval book culture: not illustrations of a text but apparently *instruments* of it. The full account is in the [Ars Notoria Field Note](https://jasoncholloway.com/field-notes/ars-notoria/).

## Condemned as cheating

The medieval world took this book seriously enough to condemn it, by the Inquisition and by Albertus Magnus. The grounds were not devil-worship. The Ars Notoria promised an illicit shortcut: knowledge acquired without the sanctioned labor of study. Condemned as cheating.

Institutions rarely mobilize against texts they consider trivial. The vehemence suggests the manuscript was circulating widely among exactly the educated class the universities were built to produce.

## How a banned book survives

Condemned books in the manuscript era did not disappear. They went expensive. Copies continued to be produced for centuries on good vellum, with the notae executed carefully, patrons paying scribes serious money for a text the authorities had ruled against. By 1657 it had crossed into print in English through Turner's translation. A text survives seven hundred years of official disapproval only if a chain of readers, generation after generation, privately concluded it was worth the risk and the price. The trilogy's Keepers are fiction. The behavior they are modeled on, quiet and well-funded and multi-generational custodianship of a condemned text, is the Ars Notoria's actual transmission history.

## What a modern reader notices

Strip away the angelic language and describe the method operationally: extended visual contemplation of complex structured figures, recitation on a fixed schedule, prescribed postures, repetition across weeks and months, a curriculum sequenced from foundational to advanced. A cognitive scientist recognizes elaborative encoding, spaced repetition, and structured retrieval practice, seven centuries before anyone had the vocabulary.

Does that mean medieval practitioners empirically discovered spaced repetition and encoded it as prayer? Maybe. Maybe it is a coincidence of forms. The resemblance is real and striking, and it is the documented foundation the fiction stands on.

## Where the fiction begins

**In the world:** the manuscript, the notae, the promise of accelerated learning, the condemnation, the surviving copies, the scholarly reappraisal.

**In the novels:** that it works, and how. Nadia's argument, developed across two volumes, is that the notae are interfaces, calibrated visual objects designed to produce specific neurological states in a prepared observer, with fasting rules, postures, and prayer sequences functioning as a protocol for reaching cortical coherence before engaging the figures. No historian makes that claim. No cognitive scientist makes it. It is the trilogy's one experiment the lab has never run.

One further invention points forward: the trilogy's claim that the notae are *frozen sound*, cymatics patterns copied from standing waves by monks who watched geometry form in water and sand. The figures are real. The mechanism is mine. See [Sound Into Form](https://jasoncholloway.com/blog/sound-into-form-hans-jenny/) and the [Cymatics Field Note](https://jasoncholloway.com/field-notes/cymatics/).

## Why this manuscript

The occult corpus is enormous. The trilogy could have run on the Lesser Key, on Agrippa, on Dee's Enochian tables, all of which sit in my research library, none of which carries the story. The Ars Notoria does, for one reason: it is the only major text in that tradition whose stated purpose is *cognition*. It doesn't summon anything. It claims to change what a mind can hold and how fast. A trilogy about perception needed an operational manual, and the thirteenth century had already written one and been condemned for it.
