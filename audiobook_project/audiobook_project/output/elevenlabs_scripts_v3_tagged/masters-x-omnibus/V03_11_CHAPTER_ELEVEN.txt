Chapter Eleven. Crane's Argument.

One hundred nine hertz. Kansas City.

[short pause]

The phone rang at 11:47 PM Kansas City time. Blake was at the kitchen table with the tenth Moleskine open and his tremor going at one oh seven point three hertz and the kettle reheating for the second time. He almost did not answer.

Father Emmanuel Crane Jr. The voice was the same voice it had been in the Strahov scriptorium five years ago, the voice that had told Blake the chamber upstairs was older than the monastery built around it, the voice that had introduced him to Eva Černá. The voice of a man who had spent his life choosing his words.

"Doctor Masters. Forgive the hour. I am six time zones west and I am operating in a pre-publication window that closes tomorrow morning."

"Father."

"I am submitting a piece to Theological Studies. The proofs went to the editor this evening. I wanted you to read it before it appeared."

"Why?"

"Because I am about to call your protocol gatekeeping prayer, and I am going to be right, and I do not want to do that to a man who once held my hand while I wept in the crypt without first calling him."

Blake said nothing.

"It is a long argument," Crane said. "I will summarise it. The acoustic frequency you and Doctor Volkov have spent ten years developing the institutional protocol around is, by your own published account, the resonant fundamental of the human chest cavity. It is the frequency every human body produces when at rest. It is the kingdom of God within. It is not learned. It is remembered. The fifty-two-week preparation protocol is therefore not a spiritual prerequisite. It is an institutional one. Every week of preparation the Foundation requires is a week during which a suffering person is denied access to their own fundamental frequency. The preparation is necessary. The preparation is prudent. But the preparation is also, in the strictest theological sense, an act of withholding grace."

The kettle clicked off. Blake did not move.

"I have spent thirty years in triage medicine, Doctor Masters. Every triage protocol in the history of medicine prioritises the urgent over the procedural. When a patient is bleeding, you do not fill out the intake form. You stop the bleeding. The Foundation's patients are bleeding. They are alienated from their own bodies, their own frequencies, their own divine architecture. And the Foundation is asking them to fill out a fifty-two-week intake form."

"You're not wrong," Blake said.

A long pause.

"I am giving you the chance," Crane said, "to write a response that the journal will publish in the same issue. Or to write a co-author's note. Or to call me a liar. I have prepared for any of those outcomes."

"I won't write a response."

"Why not?"

"Because the alternative to gatekeeping is Maryland. The alternative is Cognigenics. The alternative is three seizures and two cardiac events and a thirty-four-percent adverse event rate. The alternative is the harm done when a frequency moves faster than the body's readiness to receive it."

"That is the argument I am going to publish, Doctor Masters. That you are right about the harm and right to fear it. That the Foundation is asking the right question. And that the right question is still wrong, because the cost of asking it is borne by the people who are bleeding now while the Foundation is still asking."

"I know."

"You will not respond?"

"I will not respond."

"You understand what I am about to publish."

"Yes."

"And you are not going to argue."

"No."

There was a sound on the other end of the line that Blake recognised as Crane sitting down. The man had been standing for the duration of the call. Now he was not.

"Then I have one more thing to say to you, and I will say it as a man who has held your hand in a crypt and not as a man with an article in proof. The argument I am about to publish is not an argument against you. It is an argument that has been waiting eight hundred years to find someone competent enough to put it in print. Aldric heard it before he scattered the satchels. The Strahov monks heard it before they sealed the eight pages. You have heard it every morning since you came down out of those caves. I am not the messenger. I am the secretary."

"I know."

"Goodnight, Blake."

"Goodnight, Father."

The line went dead.

Blake sat at the kitchen table for a long time. The kettle had cooled past coffee temperature. The tremor went, one oh seven point three hertz, his fluency, his percussion. The tenth Moleskine was open in front of him, blank on the page where Crane's argument now waited to be written into the room.

[pause]

Three days later, the article appeared.

Father Emmanuel Crane Jr. published in Theological Studies. The article was titled "The Acoustic Gospel: Frequency, the Body, and the Ethics of Delayed Disclosure," and it arrived on Blake's desk (Nadia forwarded it) with the particular weight of an argument that was simultaneously wrong and irrefutable.

Crane's position was simple: if the frequency was what Jesus taught, if the kingdom of God within you was a technical description of human acoustic resonance, then the 52-week preparation protocol was gatekeeping prayer. Every week of preparation that the Foundation required was a week during which a suffering person was denied access to their own fundamental frequency. The preparation was necessary, Crane conceded. The preparation was prudent. But the preparation was also, in the strictest theological sense, an act of withholding grace.

"I have spent thirty years in triage medicine," Crane wrote. "Every triage protocol in the history of medicine prioritizes the urgent over the procedural. When a patient is bleeding, you do not fill out the intake form. You stop the bleeding. The Foundation's patients are bleeding. They are alienated from their own bodies, their own frequencies, their own divine architecture. And the Foundation is asking them to fill out a fifty-two-week intake form."

Blake read the article three times. He read it the way he read everything that frightened him, slowly, with the Breitling ticking, with coffee, with the particular attention of a man who knows that the argument he is reading is the argument he cannot dismiss because the argument is built on the same foundation he stands on.

Crane was not wrong. The preparation was gatekeeping. But the alternative was Maryland. The alternative was Cognigenics. The alternative was three seizures and two cardiac events and a 34 percent adverse event rate and a Senate hearing and a DOD classification and the entire institutional disaster that followed when knowledge moved faster than readiness.

He wrote a response. Not for Theological Studies, for himself. In the tenth Moleskine.

Crane is not wrong. The preparation IS gatekeeping. But the gate is not arbitrary. The gate is the body. The body requires time. A child possesses the capacity to drive. We do not give children keys. Not because driving is dangerous, because the child is not ready. The preparation is not about the frequency. The preparation is about the organism that will receive the frequency. The organism must be prepared. This is not theology. This is physics. This is love.

He read what he had written. The tremor making the words wobble on the page, the handwriting carrying its own frequency, the one eleven point two hertz annotation that accompanied everything Blake Masters committed to paper.

He did not publish the response. Some arguments are not for publication. Some arguments are for the Moleskine, for the desk, for the quiet hour when a man sits alone and confronts the possibility that the work he has dedicated his life to protecting is also the work that is preventing someone, somewhere, right now, from hearing the frequency that could save them.

The Breitling ticked. The coffee cooled. The argument remained unresolved. It would remain unresolved for the rest of his life, because some arguments are not meant to be resolved. They are meant to be carried. The weight of them is the preparation.
