# The Frequency That Was Already There

*The Facts Behind the Fiction, No. 1 · Jason Carroll Holloway · Seventh City Press*

Every page of the Distribution File carries the same footer: f = 111.2 Hz. Two hundred forty-seven pages, one stamp. Readers ask about it more than anything else in the trilogy. Where did the number come from? Is it real?

The number is fiction — but only by one decimal place. The stone underneath it was measured before I started writing.

## What was actually measured

Archaeoacoustics is a modest field. Researchers record how sound behaves in ancient built spaces — resonant frequencies, reverberation, standing waves — and ask whether those properties were designed or incidental. The careful practitioners make careful claims. That is why the field is worth taking seriously.

The site everyone cites is the Ħal-Saflieni Hypogeum in Malta: an underground temple complex carved from limestone around 3600–2500 BCE, now a UNESCO World Heritage Site limited to eighty visitors per day. In the Oracle Chamber, researchers have documented a pronounced resonance around 110–111 Hz. A male voice chanting in that range produces an intense reverberant effect. Cook, Pajot, and Leuchter (2008) reported a shift in prefrontal activity in subjects exposed to that resonance. One study. Small sample. That caveat belongs in every sentence that cites it, and it belongs in mine.

Malta is not alone. Paul Devereux and colleagues measured resonances at Neolithic chambers across Britain and Ireland and found peak frequencies consistently in the 95–120 Hz band. Aaron Watson and David Keating did the same at Camster Round in Scotland. Iegor Reznikoff and Michel Dauvois observed that Palaeolithic paintings at Lascaux and other French caves cluster disproportionately in zones of highest acoustic resonance — published in 1988, received by mainstream archaeology with interest and caution in roughly equal measure.

I assembled the full record — sites, studies, citations, and an FAQ — in the [111 Hz Field Note](https://jasoncholloway.com/field-notes/111-hz/). The short version: a recurring resonance near 110 Hz in ancient stone enclosures is documented. Whether anyone intended it is not.

## Why the fiction adds a decimal

Why does the trilogy say 111.2 instead of 110?

If I had written 110 Hz, I would have been borrowing the authority of the measured literature for claims the literature does not make. The archaeoacousticians measured a resonance. They did not measure Blake Masters' bilateral tremor — locked in CANON at exactly 111.2 Hz — or a preparation protocol, or a perception chamber. One decimal past the real number is the fiction signing its own work. Close enough to honor the source. Distinct enough that nobody mistakes my invention for their measurement.

Precision is also a character trait. William Masters is an engineer. A man who leaves seven leather notebooks for his grandson does not write "about 110." He writes 111.2, with the instrumentation to defend the second decimal.

## The frequency ladder

The trilogy's frequency system is a ladder. Every rung has a real referent — which is why each one needs a label.

**7.83 Hz** — the Schumann resonance, the fundamental of the cavity between Earth's surface and the ionosphere. Textbook geophysics. *In the world.*

**110 Hz** — the archaeoacoustic band above. *In the world*, with the caveat that the literature is thinner than a thriller needs it to be.

**111.2 Hz** — the trilogy's carrier frequency, Blake's tremor, the Distribution File footer. *In the novels.*

**136.6 Hz** — connected in the books to transcendence states, mapped to the Monroe Institute's focus-level framework. The U.S. Army assessed that framework in the Gateway Process document — a real declassified file, and its own essay. The traditions are documented. The claims inside them are not scientific findings. *Contested at best.*

Chapter headers map this ladder onto real places — the Kansas City limestone under [SubTropolis](https://jasoncholloway.com/field-notes/subtropolis/), the Strahov crypt in Prague, Icelandic basalt. Every referent is documented. The claim that they form one coherent system is the novel. *The rungs are real. The ladder is fiction.*

## Sound has shapes

One more piece of real science holds the frequency system up. In 1787, Ernst Chladni drew a violin bow along a sand-covered metal plate and showed that sand migrates to the nodal lines — the places the plate is still — forming geometric figures that change with frequency. Hans Jenny extended the work in the 1960s: shift from 800 to 865 cycles per second and the pattern does not adjust — it transforms into a categorically different figure.

In a universe where sound produces form, 111.2 is not interchangeable with 111.3. The [Cymatics Field Note](https://jasoncholloway.com/field-notes/cymatics/) covers Chladni and Jenny in full, including where popular cymatics claims outrun the research.

## Why label any of this

The trilogy is about the danger of ungraded patterns. Umberto Eco's warning — that pattern-hunters manufacture the pattern they seek — runs through everything Blake Masters does. A book built on that warning cannot hand its readers an unmarked mixture of measurement and invention.

So: the Schumann resonance is in the world. The 110 Hz chamber literature is in the world, thin but real. The 111.2 Hz carrier, the tremor, the protocol, the chamber — in the novels.

The research layer is live in the [Field Notes](https://jasoncholloway.com/field-notes/). The books are at [jasoncholloway.com/books/masters-x/](https://jasoncholloway.com/books/masters-x/).

The facts are in the files. The fiction is in the books.
