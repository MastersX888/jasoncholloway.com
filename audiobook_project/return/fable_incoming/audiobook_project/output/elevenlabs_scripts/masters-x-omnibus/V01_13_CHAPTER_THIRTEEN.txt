Chapter Thirteen. Prague.

One eleven point two hertz. Prague.


The city hit Blake like a resonance he could almost hear.

They came through customs together. Nadia moving with the ease of someone who'd passed through Václav Havel Airport a dozen times, Blake trailing in her wake, his perception already flaring from the terminal's modernist architecture.

"Stay close," Nadia said, taking his arm. "Blake. Stay with me."

"I'm here."

"You're not. You're reading the ceiling. Look at me." She turned his face toward hers. Her eyes. Dark, steady. "We get our bags. We get a taxi. We get to the hotel. Then you can read the whole city. But right now. Bags, taxi, hotel. Yes?"

"Yes."

She led him through the terminal like she was guiding a man through a snowstorm, which, perceptually, she was.

The taxi ride into Old Town broke him open.

Blake pressed his forehead against the window and watched Prague unfold. Gothic spires and Baroque facades, each building triggering responses in his awareness he didn't have names for. The architectural language was overwhelming. Every structure spoke in dimensional ratios, every plaza opened into calculated proportions.

"It's all encoded," Blake whispered.

"Every stone," Nadia confirmed. "Charles IV hired architects who knew the system. Rudolf the Second expanded it. Three centuries of deliberate mathematical encoding, built into the city's bones."

"Breathe," she said.

"I am breathing."

"You're holding your breath. Every time a new building hits your vision, you stop breathing." She reached across the seat, put her hand flat over his heartbeat. "Your heart rate is elevated. Use the third figura. Narrow the aperture."

Blake narrowed. The patterns dimmed to manageable levels. Prague became a city instead of a three-dimensional diagram.

"You've done this before. Guided someone through Prague."

A pause. "Twice."

"What happened to them?"

"They weren't you."

The hotel was on a narrow street near the Powder Tower. Small, unremarkable. Nadia greeted the desk clerk by name, spoke Czech with an ease that reminded Blake she'd been trained for this world since childhood. Two rooms. Adjoining. Third floor.

Nadia knocked on the adjoining door. He opened it.

She stood in the doorway between their rooms. "I'm going to shower and change. Miroslav expects us at six. You should sleep."

"I won't be able to sleep."

"Try. Two hours." She studied his face. "The first candidate I brought here. Elena, Russian, extraordinarily gifted. She stood at that same window and cried. Not from sadness. From recognition."

"What happened to Elena?"

Nadia's expression closed. "She went into the Strahov chamber. She saw the complete system. And she couldn't come back. She's in a care facility outside Moscow."

Blake felt the cold settle into his chest. "And the second candidate?"

"Markus chose not to enter the chamber. He teaches mathematics in Vienna. Married. Two children." She met Blake's eyes. "Both were valid choices."

"But you brought me here."

"Because you're different from both of them. Elena had the gift but not the preparation. Markus had the preparation but not the courage." She stepped through the doorway. Into his room. "You have both. And you have something else."

"What?"

"A reason to come back." She held his gaze. Then she stepped backward, back into her room. "Sleep. Two hours. I'll knock."

The door closed. Blake lay on the bed. Prague's geometric language pulsed through the walls. And he found Nadia's resonance. Her voice, her hand on his chest in the taxi. And narrowed to that.

He slept.



Miroslav Khoury lived above a bookshop on Nerudova Street, the steep cobblestone road climbing from Malá Strana toward the castle. Nadia led Blake through streets she navigated without checking her phone. Her territory. Her language. Her world.

Blake watched her move through Prague and understood something. In America, Nadia had been the outsider. The European scholar adapting to a Midwestern city. Here, she was native. The way she turned corners without looking. The way she touched the stone walls as she climbed. Fingertips against medieval masonry, the same gesture she used on manuscript pages. Reading the city through her skin.

"You're different here," Blake said.

She glanced back. "How?"

"More yourself."

Something moved through her expression. She turned and kept climbing.

Miroslav opened the door before they knocked. Eighty-seven years old, thin and precise. His apartment was a library with a kitchen attached. He greeted Nadia in Czech, embraced her, then turned to Blake.

"You have William's face," he said in accented English. "But your mother's coloring. Margaret, yes?"

"You knew her?"

"I knew OF her. William described her in his letters. The woman who kept him human when the patterns tried to take him somewhere else." Miroslav's eyes moved to Nadia, then back to Blake. "History echoes."

Tea. Small pastries. And wine. A Moravian Riesling, pale gold, from a vineyard outside Mikulov that Miroslav said the Jesuits had planted in 1617.

Nadia took the wine. Blake noticed. In Kansas City she'd chosen tea over coffee, coffee over wine. Coffee is for work. Wine is for honesty. Here, in Prague, she chose wine. Chose honesty.

"The vineyard survived the Communists," Miroslav said. "Because the monks buried the rootstock. Grafted it back after 1989. The same vines, three hundred and seventy years old." He looked at Blake. "Everything worth preserving requires an act of burial. You bury them to save them. And then you wait for the world to become safe enough to dig them up."

Blake opened his perception. The narrow aperture. He looked at the manuscript under glass on Miroslav's table and read it. The dimensional relationships, the mathematical language underneath the medieval decoration.

"This is a synthesis document," Blake said. "From Rudolf's court. Late sixteenth century."

"Stop." Miroslav's hand was raised. His eyes were wet. "William did the same thing. Stood where you're standing. Said almost the same words." He wiped his eyes. "Forgive an old man. I've been waiting forty years for this."

"What happened when William saw it?" Nadia asked quietly.

"He wanted to go further. Into the chamber beneath Strahov." Miroslav looked at Blake. "I gave him the key. He went to the entrance. And he couldn't do it."

"Why not?"

"Because he was alone." Miroslav's gaze moved to Nadia. "He didn't have anyone to come back to. Margaret was in Kansas City. James was a child."

"When?" Blake asked.

"Tomorrow. Dawn. The library is empty before the staff arrives. Two hours in the chamber." Miroslav walked them to the door.

At the threshold: "Your grandfather asked me to tell you one thing. He said: 'The chamber shows you everything. The gift lets you see it. But only love lets you survive it.'" The old man's eyes were luminous. "William understood that too late."

They walked down Nerudova Street in the October dusk, the castle looming above them.

"I'm thinking about Elena," Nadia said.

"I'm not Elena."

"I know." She stopped on the cobblestones. "But I'm the one who brought her here. Taught her the circuit breakers. Told her she was ready. And I was wrong."

"You were wrong about her preparation. Not about the method."

"If that happens to you -"

"You'll pull me back. The way you pulled me back in the vault."

"What if I can't?"

Blake took her hand. Deliberately. On a medieval street in Prague with the castle above them and the river below.

"Then you call my name. And I shall find your frequency. You're my circuit breaker, Nadia. Not the seventh figura. You."

They walked back to the hotel holding hands. Through the Old Town Square. Past the Powder Tower.

At the hotel, they stopped outside their rooms. Adjacent doors.

"Tomorrow," Blake said.

"Tomorrow."

Nadia paused. "Blake, your grandfather was wrong about one thing. He said he didn't have anyone to come back to. But he did. He had Margaret. He just couldn't see past the patterns to find her." She held his gaze. "Don't lose me in the patterns. Please."

"I won't."

She went into her room. Blake stood in the hallway, Prague humming through the walls, the Strahov key in his pocket, Nadia's frequency still warm in his hand.

Blake opened his door. Lay down in the dark. Reached her frequency.

And held it until morning.
