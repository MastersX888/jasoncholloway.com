## CHARTRES CATHEDRAL

**In the novels.** First stop of Blake's European measurement circuit — Vol. III ch. 8, "The Stone Voices," chapter frequency **111.2 Hz · Chartres**. "A Tuesday in March. The cathedral was empty… Blake was alone in Chartres Cathedral, sitting cross-legged on the 12th-century floor, his spectrum analyzer recording, his Moleskine open, his hands trembling at 111.2 Hz, his hands adding their own frequency to the room." His Moleskine verdict: **"Chartres: 108 Hz. The rib vaults are mirrors. The Gothic builders did what Moreau did, they used the architecture to concentrate natural resonance. But Moreau used metal. The builders used stone."** The visit ends at the labyrinth — "12.9 meters in diameter, set into the nave floor in the same Berchères limestone, the path a single route from the entrance to the center, no dead ends, no choices" (*Omnibus*, Vol. III ch. 8). William had already mapped the site decades earlier: one of the model airplanes in his garage hung at "the height that corresponds to Chartres" (*Omnibus*, Vol. II, Iceland cottage scene). The Distribution File carries the number forward as data: Limestone (Chartres), natural fundamental 108.0 Hz, deviation −3.2 Hz, Q 94, "cathedral measurement" (*DF*, Substrate Tables and site cross-check table).

**In the world.** Chartres is real and superlatively documented: the post-1194-fire High Gothic rebuild, the labyrinth, the cobalt blue glass, the asymmetric spires. The trilogy's acoustic reading rides genuine scholarship recovered in the V3 salvage pass: Otto von Simson, *The Gothic Cathedral* (1956) — the classic argument that Gothic proportion was theology built in stone; Christopher Wilson, *The Gothic Cathedral* (1990); and John James, *The Contractors of Chartres* (1979), whose reconstruction of the building campaign supports the novel's "multiple master builders working in coordination" texture. The 108 Hz figure itself is fiction's assignment, sitting deliberately adjacent to the documented 110–112 Hz archaeoacoustics band (Cook et al. 2008, `output/CITATIONS_ADDENDUM.md`; `research_synthesis/CYMATICS_RESEARCH_SYNTHESIS.md`).

**Cross-references:** The Five-Site Frequency Survey · Reims Cathedral · 111.2 Hz · SubTropolis & the Moreau Chamber · Moleskine Notebooks.

## REIMS CATHEDRAL

**In the novels.** Second cathedral, "two days later. Two days on a train… Blake arrived in Reims in early morning. Bakeries open, streets empty. The cathedral was a presence before it was a destination. The Reims bakers had been baking beneath it for eight centuries. They did not look up at it. They lived inside its acoustic footprint" (*Omnibus*, Vol. III ch. 8). "Different from Chartres. Not in kind, in proportion" — a coronation church, "the building where French kings had been anointed since the 11th century… the nave wider," scaled for royal ceremony. Reims returns the survey's anomaly: **111.8 Hz — the only site above the Masters fundamental** (deviation +0.6 Hz, Q 139, per *DF* substrate tables), the number Blake turns over on the train, unable "to decide whether the number proved the theory or the theory produced the number."

**In the world.** Reims Cathedral and its coronation history are documented fact; the frequency is fiction. Blake's train-seat epistemology — does the number prove the theory or does the theory produce the number? — is the trilogy's core methodological anxiety, calibrated directly against *Foucault's Pendulum*'s warning about invented Plans (`research_synthesis/UMBERTO_ECO_MASTERS_REFERENCE.md`) and the PKD doubt-cycle (`research_synthesis/PKD_EXEGESIS_MASTERS_REFERENCE.md`). The acoustic-physics frame for why two limestone naves would differ in fundamental (material density, dimension, Q) is real acoustics: Benade, *Fundamentals of Musical Acoustics* (1976) and Rossing, *The Science of Sound* (2014) — both recovered from the V3 annotation layer.

**Cross-references:** Chartres Cathedral · The Five-Site Frequency Survey · 111.2 Hz · The Distribution File.

## UPPSALA SHIP BURIALS

**In the novels.** Third site; three days after Reims. "A bus from Stockholm, three hours through Swedish forest… He was not measuring a cathedral this time. He was measuring a grave. The Uppsala ship burials, the royal graves of the Vendel Period, 6th to 8th century, pre-Christian Scandinavia. The graves were underground chambers: stone-lined, ship-shaped" (*Omnibus*, Vol. III ch. 8). Access is arranged by Professor Astrid Lindblom of Uppsala University. The chamber logic is stated in the trilogy's plainest terms: "They sat in the stone chamber with the body and the horn and the frequency, and the chamber did what chambers do: it reflected, it amplified, it held." Survey value: granite at **109.0 Hz** (deviation −2.2 Hz, *DF* substrate tables). Uppsala also has a first-volume shadow: Aldric's superior lists it among the dispersal cities — "Uppsala, where the old gods are not quite forgotten" (*Omnibus*, prologue) — so the site is both a thirteenth-century hiding place and a twenty-first-century measurement.

**In the world.** The Vendel Period (c. 550–790 CE) and its Uppland boat-grave cemeteries are real archaeology, and Gamla Uppsala's royal mounds are among Scandinavia's most studied monuments. The trilogy's move — reading a pre-Christian burial chamber as the same acoustic instrument as a Gothic nave — is its five-cultures argument in miniature: "Five cultures that never communicated. The same frequency in the stone" (*Omnibus*, Vol. III ch. 8). That cross-cultural convergence claim is the fictional extension of the documented-but-contested archaeoacoustics record (`research_synthesis/CYMATICS_RESEARCH_SYNTHESIS.md`; Cook et al. 2008). Specific chamber measurements at Uppsala: fiction.

**Cross-references:** Lindblom, Professor Astrid · The Five-Site Frequency Survey · Aldric, Brother · Chartres Cathedral.

## MONTSERRAT & BARCELONA

**In the novels.** The Iberian leg. Barcelona contributes the survey's modern outlier — the Sagrada Família, sandstone/basalt at 110.3 Hz (*DF* site table) — and Montserrat contributes the survey's oldest instrument type: "Not the monastery, the cave. The Santa Cova, the Holy Cave, halfway up the mountain. The funicular railway carried them up the conglomerate cliff face. Slow. The destination was not a place you rushed" (*Omnibus*, Vol. III). The Moleskine synthesis is written "on the train back to Barcelona, the Mediterranean visible from the window in flat grey sheets": **"Chartres, Reims, Uppsala, Barcelona, Montserrat. Five sites. Five countries. Five geologies. Five cultures that never communicated. The same frequency in the stone. Limestone at 108. Limestone at 111.8. Granite at 109. Basalt at 110.3. C[onglomerate]…"** (*Omnibus*, Vol. III ch. 8). The Montserrat passage also carries the trilogy's trust question in its purest form: the difference between the fifty-two-week protocol and Vance's compressed timeline is "not a difference in intention but a difference in trust. How much did you trust the container? How much did you trust the stone?"

**In the world.** Montserrat's conglomerate massif, the Santa Cova shrine, and the funicular are real; the Sagrada Família is real and famously still under construction. Every frequency value is fiction, plotted into the real 110 Hz research band. The "trust the container" formulation is the fictional restatement of the preparation-versus-shortcut divide that structures the whole research corpus — from the Ars Notoria's ritual timelines (`research_synthesis/CYMATICS_RESEARCH_SYNTHESIS.md`, notae thesis) to the Gateway Process's staged training (`research_synthesis/COMPREHENSIVE_RESEARCH_SYNTHESIS.md`).

**Cross-references:** The Five-Site Frequency Survey · Fifty-Two-Week Protocol · Vance, Dr. Helena · Chartres Cathedral.

## MERAMEC CAVERNS

**In the novels.** The trilogy's earliest chamber — Blake's origin scene. "Blake was ten years old. His grandfather stood beside him in the darkness. Meramec Caverns. Tourist attraction outside Sullivan, Missouri. But William had found a side passage, always did, everywhere they went" (*Omnibus*, Vol. I flashback). William traces the flowstone formations with his fingers; seventeen years later Blake recognizes the same shapes "in his grandfather's notebooks. In the carvings beneath SubTropolis. In the flowstone at Meramec Caverns that William had traced with his fingers seventeen years ago" (*Omnibus*, Vol. I ch. 4). William's letter names it as part of Blake's lifelong assessment: "watching how you see things others miss. The cardinal on the branch. The model airplanes I hung in the garage. The patterns in flowstone at Meramec Caverns" (*Omnibus*, Vol. I, William's letter). *(Note: this "seventeen years" is the phrase the notebook-count error was misread from — see `output/VERIFICATION_LOG.md`. William's notebooks number seven.)*

**In the world.** Meramec Caverns is a real show cave outside Sullivan, Missouri, in the same karst limestone province the trilogy builds everything on. The deeper research layer, recovered from the V3 salvage pass, is Missouri's documented cave-art record: Diaz-Granados & Duncan, *The Petroglyphs and Pictographs of Missouri* (2000) and Missouri Archaeological Society records — the state's genuine tradition of marked underground spaces, which the trilogy fictionalizes into the SubTropolis carvings. Flowstone patterning itself is real speleothem morphology; William's readable "patterns" in it are fiction.

**Cross-references:** SubTropolis & the Moreau Chamber · Masters, William · Masters, Blake · The Ozark Lake.

## ADAM-ONDI-AHMAN

**In the novels.** Not named in the trilogy — the omnibus never uses the phrase (verified by fulltext search; cf. the Temple Lot finding in `output/VERIFICATION_LOG.md`). The site operates instead as gravitational background: it is part of why the trilogy's Missouri is the kind of place where three factions converge, where "the ground itself is significant."

**In the world.** This is a research-layer entry, and the research is real. Adam-ondi-Ahman is an actual site in Daviess County, Missouri, roughly seventy miles north of Independence, identified in Latter-day Saint theology as the place where Adam gathered his descendants after Eden, and as the future location of a "grand council" at which all prophets across history will assemble before the Second Coming — a place named, in the tradition, in the Adamic language itself (`research_synthesis/INDEPENDENCE_ZION_THEOLOGICAL_INTEGRATION.md`). Together with Joseph Smith's 1831 designation of Independence as Zion's "center place" (D&C 57) and the 63-acre Temple Lot dedicated August 3, 1831, it makes western Missouri the only ground in America that a major religious tradition has mapped as both Eden and Endpoint. The trilogy's whole geographic thesis — a Kansas City corridor where five traditions independently declared the ground significant (Hopewell, Osage, Smith's Zion, the Community of Christ temple, Billings' underground compound) — takes this site as one of its five load-bearing anchors. The encyclopedia includes it because the fiction is unintelligible without it, and flags plainly: **no scene in the trilogy takes place there.**

**Cross-references:** Independence & the Temple Lot · Kansas City & Quality Hill · The Ground Itself Is Significant (Part Three, Essay 2).
