# Sound Into Form: What Hans Jenny Actually Proved

*The Facts Behind the Fiction, No. 3 · Jason Carroll Holloway · Seventh City Press*

The oldest special effect in my trilogy requires no computer. A metal plate, a violin bow, a spoonful of fine sand. Draw the bow along the plate's edge and the sand jumps into a symmetrical geometric figure — as if the tone had drawn it. The demonstration is from 1787. You can reproduce it on your kitchen table tonight: level the plate, sift the sand, keep the bow pressure light.

Everything strange in the Masters X universe stands on that plate. What the plate proves is one thing. What the novels build on top of it is another, and the two are worth keeping apart.

## Chladni: frequency becomes figure

Ernst Chladni (1756–1827) documented the phenomenon systematically in his 1787 *Entdeckungen über die Theorie des Klanges*. A bowed plate vibrates in modes; at a resonant frequency, some regions move violently while the nodal lines stay still. Sand bounces off the moving regions and collects on the still ones. The figure you see is a map of the silence — the geometry of where the plate is *not* moving. Change the frequency and the figure changes.

*Measured.* Reproducible by anyone. Disputed by no one.

## Jenny: one phenomenon, three registers

Hans Jenny (1904–1972), a Swiss physician, took Chladni's parlor demonstration and turned it into a research program. Crystal oscillators replaced violin bows. Liquids, pastes, and powders replaced sand. He published the results in the two-volume *Kymatik* (1967, 1972) — translated as *Cymatics*, the term he coined.

Two findings matter for the fiction. First, sensitivity: a small frequency change can produce a categorically different pattern — Jenny documented a shift from 800 to 865 cycles per second that transforms the figure rather than adjusting it. In a world where sound makes form, the decimal places matter. That is why 111.2 Hz cannot be treated as interchangeable with 111 or 112.

Second, conditionality. Turn the tone off and the pattern collapses. Turn it back on and the *same* pattern returns. Order is not built and then left standing; it is maintained by the frequency. Jenny's summary — "one can hear what one sees" — names his triad: the wave you hear, the pattern you see, the periodicity you feel, one phenomenon apprehended three ways.

What Jenny did not prove — and the [Cymatics Field Note](https://jasoncholloway.com/field-notes/cymatics/) draws this boundary explicitly — is anything about healing frequencies, water memory, or consciousness. The physics is real. A good deal of what circulates under the word "cymatics" outruns it.

## Where the record ends

There is no evidence that *viewing* a cymatics pattern alters consciousness. The documented phenomenon is sound organizing matter. The trilogy's central conceit is that the resulting geometry, contemplated by a prepared observer, does something to the observer. That claim is entirely in the novels.

The trilogy proposes that medieval practitioners, chanting daily for decades in resonant stone over water basins, saw standing-wave patterns form and copied them — that the Ars Notoria's notae are cymatics figures, frozen sound — and that a scribe in Bohemia in 1267 fixed seven hundred twelve of them onto vellum without knowing what he was drawing. Every clause of that is mine. No historian makes the claim.

*Sound makes form* is measured. *Form remakes the observer* is invented.

## Water

Jenny filmed liquids as well as powders, and liquids are where his footage is most arresting — standing patterns holding on a vibrating water surface. The research note in my files that became a structural principle is one sentence long: water amplifies cymatics effects.

The trilogy keeps returning to water: Brother Aldric's basin in the 1267 prologue, Father Moreau's river, the Ozark lake with its ninety documented feet of dark. In the novels, water is the medium in which the frequency becomes visible enough to copy.

In *The Grimoire*, Nadia maps the Strahov chamber's resonance into a stable hexagonal figure, then watches footage of Kofi Asante's drumming in Ghana producing a five-sided standing wave in red laterite clay. Both specific patterns, and Nadia's inference that they share a structural origin, are invented. The dependence of pattern on material, geometry, and frequency — the thing that makes her inference feel like science — is real. The Ghana cave acoustics are labeled fiction in the Field Notes.

## Yuki Tanaka

The trilogy's researchers do not merely cite cymatics; they practice it. Yuki Tanaka's disc experiments — controlled frequency, documented media, repeated trials, one variable at a time — are fictional experiments conducted correctly. The characters' method is real method. Only their results are invented.

Hans Jenny gives the Masters X universe not proof of anything mystical but a demonstration — bench-top, repeatable, gorgeous — that frequency and form are already married in the physical world. The novels propose one further wedding, and they say so out loud.

The facts are in the files. The fiction is in the books.
