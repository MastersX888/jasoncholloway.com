## MASTERS, MARGARET

**In the novels.** Blake's mother; sixty-three when the trilogy's present-day action begins, answering her door "in an apron dusted with flour" to a son who hadn't called ahead (*Omnibus*, Vol. I ch. 8, "The Key" — chapter frequency 109 Hz, Kansas City). Her house "smelled like bread and lavender… the smell that meant safety." She serves tea, not coffee, when she wants a real conversation. Keeper of the family's most consequential object: the key James entrusted to her the night before his death — "Said if anything happened, to hide this. Give it to you when you were ready" (*Omnibus*, Vol. I ch. 8). The trilogy later reveals a second Margaret entirely: her private correspondence with William named the trait the Foundation would spend twelve years learning to assess — she called it **"the perceptual fingerprint"** (*Omnibus*, Vol. III, Yuki Tanaka assessment scene). Miroslav Khoury, who knew her only through William's letters, describes her as "the woman who kept him human when the patterns tried to take him somewhere else" (*Omnibus*, Vol. II, Nerudova Street scene).

**In the world.** Margaret carries the trilogy's domestic register — the counterweight the research library builds against abstraction. Her structural function (the generational keeper of objects and thresholds: key, watch, kitchen table) parallels the caretaker figures in the Billings-parallel material, where the visionary's family absorbs the cost of the vision (`research_synthesis/ROGER_BILLINGS_MASTERS_X_ANALYSIS.md`). Her naming of "the perceptual fingerprint" before the Foundation existed makes her, quietly, the universe's first assessment scientist.

**Cross-references:** Masters, James · Masters, Blake · Masters, Ethan · Breitling Navitimer · Khoury, Miroslav.

## MASTERS, ETHAN

**In the novels.** Blake's nephew, thirty-one; "practical, the kind of person who fixed things with his hands while Blake fixed them with his mind. Ethan had his father's build and his grandmother's steadiness" (*Omnibus*, Vol. I ch. 8). Present in Margaret's kitchen when Blake comes asking about Oscar-01; his pragmatism "slic[es] through ambiguity": "You fell in a library and you're wearing Grandpa's watch and you look like you haven't slept in three days. That's not careful." His line — **"I'm tired of going to Masters funerals, Blake"** — is the family's whole cost accounting in eight words: "not anger, not even fear. Exhaustion" (*Omnibus*, Vol. I ch. 8).

**In the world.** Ethan is the trilogy's answer to a documented pattern in the research corpus: what the gift does to the people who don't have it. The Billings analysis and the PKD *Exegesis* material both track the collateral exhaustion of families orbiting an obsessive pattern-seeker (`research_synthesis/ROGER_BILLINGS_MASTERS_X_ANALYSIS.md`; `research_synthesis/PKD_EXEGESIS_MASTERS_REFERENCE.md`). Ethan is that research finding given a Sports section and a chair at the kitchen table.

**Cross-references:** Masters, Margaret · Masters, Blake · Masters, James.

## MORALES, TERESA

**In the novels.** The whistleblower. Mid-fifties, "gray thre[aded]" hair, a brown leather bag "that looks like it's been on a lot of planes" — she arrives at the Foundation's lobby without an appointment, and Nadia, who has been expecting her without knowing her name, says "Send her up" (*Omnibus*, Vol. III, Foundation lobby scene). She **"walked out of Cognigenics with documents that proved Vance knew the seizure risk"** and delivers them to Senator Holt's committee — "the most surgically precise testimony the committee had heard in the current session" (*Omnibus*, Vol. III, Senate hearing). On the phone she is "compressed, a voice delivering information… organized by threat level"; in person, expansive. Seated beside Nadia at the hearing, she is narrated as "the other frequency" — the trilogy's image for two women carrying the same signal by different paths.

**In the world.** Teresa's document walk maps onto the documented anatomy of institutional whistleblowing — the insider who exfiltrates evidence of known risk concealed for schedule or profit. The Foundation/Cognigenics split she exposes is the fictionalization of the three-camp debate (suppress / preserve / weaponize) recorded in the 1984 CIA translation of Chinese parapsychology research: `research_synthesis/COMPREHENSIVE_RESEARCH_SYNTHESIS.md` §I.2. Her employer's missing research file is a live editorial issue — see `OPEN_DECISIONS.md` (Cognigenics.txt) — and this entry deliberately cites none of it.

**Cross-references:** Vance, Dr. Helena · The Completion Sect & Cognigenics · Holt, Senator Margaret · Volkov, Nadia.

## LINDQVIST, EVA

**In the novels.** A protocol practitioner in Copenhagen — one of the Foundation's dispersed post-release network. Her single appearance is a 5:44 AM text exchange with Nadia (12:44 PM in Copenhagen): *"Did you run the protocol this morning? I did it in the taxi. Driver thought I was asleep."* Nadia's answer — "Yes. First time. Fifteen minutes." — marks the scene where the Foundation's architect finally runs her own protocol (*Omnibus*, Vol. III). Lindqvist exists in the trilogy as proof that the practice has left the building: it now lives in taxis, time zones, and text threads.

**In the world.** The one-scene character is a deliberate scale device — the narrative equivalent of the Distribution File's adoption statistics (1,204,881 week-one downloads; three independent replications: *DF*, front matter). Not to be confused with **Eva Černá** (Charles University; see her Draft 1 entry) — the trilogy has two Evas, one inside the institutions and one outside them.

**Cross-references:** Volkov, Nadia · The Distribution File · Fifty-Two-Week Protocol · Černá, Eva.

## KHOURY, MIROSLAV

**In the novels.** Eighty-seven years old, "thin and precise," living above a bookshop on **Nerudova Street** — "the steep cobblestone road climbing from Malá Strana toward the castle" (*Omnibus*, Vol. II, Prague arc). "His apartment was a library with a kitchen attached." He opens the door before Blake and Nadia knock; greets Nadia in Czech; recognizes Blake's face on sight — "your mother's coloring. Margaret, yes?… I knew OF her. William described her in his letters." His verdict on the Masters generations: **"History echoes."** He serves tea, small pastries, and a Moravian Riesling "from a vineyard outside Mikulov that Miroslav said the Jesuits had planted in 1617" — the glass of wine Nadia accepts in Prague after refusing it in Kansas City ("Coffee is for work. Wine is for honesty").

**In the world.** Khoury is the trilogy's living archive — the correspondent generation that connects William's 1965 Strahov visit to Blake's return. Nerudova Street and Malá Strana are real Prague geography; the Jesuit viticultural detail rides the same institutional-continuity research as the Specchi commission and the Georgetown material (`research_synthesis/JESUIT_GEORGETOWN_CIA_RESEARCH.md`; `research_synthesis/JESUIT_BOOKS_USAGE_GUIDE.md`). Moravian wine country around Mikulov is real; the specific 1617 Jesuit planting is novel texture `[NEEDS SOURCE — cite a Moravian viticulture history or mark as invention if the encyclopedia repeats the date]`. Prague as a refuge for hidden knowledge under Rudolf II — the magnet court for "alchemists, astronomers, and mystics" — is documented in Findlen, *Possessing Nature* (1994), a link recovered from the V3 annotation layer.

**Cross-references:** Strahov Monastery & Crypt · Volkov, Nadia · Masters, William · Masters, Margaret · The Specchi Mirrors.

## LINDBLOM, PROFESSOR ASTRID

**In the novels.** Uppsala University, Department of Archaeology; "Blake's age, mid-forties, fine-boned, with the particular directness of Scandinavian academics" (*Omnibus*, Vol. III, Uppsala chapter). She grants Blake access to the Vendel-era ship burials — the only measurement site in the five-site survey that requires an institutional gatekeeper rather than an open cathedral door. Her presence is the trilogy quietly conceding a real-world fact: you do not measure a royal grave field with a spectrum analyzer without a professor signing you in.

**In the world.** Uppsala University is real and its archaeology department is the natural custodian for Vendel Period material; Lindblom herself is fiction. The academic-access texture — permissions, supervision, the tension between "tourists carry cameras and scientists carry things with screens" (*Omnibus*, Vol. III) — is the fieldwork reality behind every archaeoacoustics study in the research corpus (`research_synthesis/CYMATICS_RESEARCH_SYNTHESIS.md`; Cook et al. 2008 in `output/CITATIONS_ADDENDUM.md`).

**Cross-references:** Uppsala Ship Burials · The Five-Site Frequency Survey · Masters, Blake.
