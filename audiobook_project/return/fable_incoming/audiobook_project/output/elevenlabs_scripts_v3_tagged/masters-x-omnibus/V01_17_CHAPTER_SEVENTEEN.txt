Chapter Seventeen. The Conference.

One eleven point two hertz. Prague.

[short pause]

Nadia presented first.

The International Symposium on Medieval Manuscript Studies. Conference Room C. Grand Hotel Bohemia. Sixty scholars, morning light through tall windows, the astronomical clock visible across the Old Town Square.

She stood at the podium in her professional armor. Dark blazer, manuscript catalog in hand. No ring. She'd taken it off that morning without comment.

"For seven centuries," Nadia began, "these three manuscript traditions have been studied independently. What I'm presenting today is evidence that they are fragments of a single system, deliberately separated in 1267 by Brother Aldric."

She clicked through the historical framework. Manuscript provenance, transmission records, Rudolf the Second's kunstkammer. Meticulous scholarship. Every claim sourced.

The room listened. Blake watched the scholars' faces. Professional recognition, slow nods, sharper attention where the framework threatened published work.

"The separation was intentional," she continued. "Aldric's solution was fragmentation. For seven hundred years, that scholar didn't exist."

She paused. Looked at Blake.

"Now one does."

Blake walked to the podium. Nadia stepped aside but stayed at the edge of the stage.

"I'm not a manuscript scholar," Blake said. "Doctor Volkov has established the historical framework. What I can show you is the pattern that connects them."

He clicked to the overlays. Three images side by side. Then the geometric overlay. The seven-fold structure emerging across all three, proportional relationships matching to four decimal places.

The room went quiet.

A hand in the third row. Doctor Klaus Bergmann, Berlin. "Are you suggesting a common source?"

"I'm demonstrating one." Blake clicked to the cave photographs. "Pre-Columbian site in Missouri. Same geometric sequences. Predating the European manuscripts by centuries."

Nadia stepped forward beside Blake. "The transmission path is documented in Rudolf the Second's court records." She cited the sources, gave the call numbers. The historical evidence supporting what Blake had shown them.

Two ways of reading. Together. In front of sixty witnesses.

Professor Sarah Chen from Cambridge stood. "The proportional system. May I see it again?" She studied the geometric relationships. "We documented every measurement. But we never connected it to the seven-fold structure because -"

"Because you were looking at each manuscript in isolation," Nadia said. "The pattern only emerges in synthesis."

Chen looked between them. "If this holds up, this rewrites five hundred years of scholarship."

"It will hold up," Nadia said. "The sources are verifiable."

The presentation ran ninety minutes. Scholars swarmed the podium afterward, business cards, invitations to present at Cambridge, Heidelberg, the Sorbonne.

And in the back, half-hidden behind a pillar, Father Crane watched.

[pause]

Crane found them in the courtyard café. He sat without being invited.

"Impressive presentation. Your grandfather would have been proud." He turned to Blake. "I'm not here to suppress you. I'm here because of this." He opened a folder. A photograph of a young man's face twisted in anguish. "Thomas Crane. My younger brother. The Completion Sect recruited him in 1988. Forced the enhancement without preparation. Institutionalized for thirty-seven years."

"That's what happens when the knowledge spreads too fast," Crane said. "When people without the preparation try to reproduce what you demonstrated."

"Suppression has destroyed more," Nadia said. "Your order has been suppressing for seven centuries. The knowledge didn't go away. It went underground. Became more dangerous."

Crane studied the space between them. "You're in love with him," he said to Nadia.

"That's not relevant to the scholarship."

"It's relevant to everything. Love makes people reckless." He stood. "Think about what happens to the people who try to follow where Mr. Masters leads."

He left the folder. Walked away.

"He's not wrong," Nadia said. "About the casualties."

"He's not wrong about the risk. He's wrong about the solution."

[pause]

Doctor Helena Vance found Blake alone. Hotel bar. Corner booth.

"The Completion Sect," Blake said before she could introduce herself.

"Research consortium." Vance ordered whiskey. "Your presentation was the most significant thing I've seen in twenty years. We want you to help us improve the protocols. Your natural abilities, it's what we've been trying to reproduce."

"With a twenty-nine percent failure rate."

"Phase III is at twelve percent. And the failures are manageable."

"Except for the twelve percent who break."

"Every medical advancement has costs." Vance leaned forward. "Imagine the full perception available to everyone. Every scientist. Every artist."

"I'll think about it."

"Don't think too long. The conference ends tomorrow."

She left. Blake sat with two untouched whiskeys and two business cards.

He texted Nadia: Where are you?

Hotel room. Transcribing. Are you okay?

Vance just made her pitch. Same as Crane. Different packaging.

Come back. We'll work through it together.

Blake walked back to the hotel. Nadia was sitting cross-legged on her bed surrounded by Latin transcriptions.

"The Third Way," he said. "That's the answer. Not suppression. Not acceleration. Voluntary awakening. But the question you asked - how do you offer without destroying. That has to be the center."

"The control tower," she said. "Someone who can guide people safely. The perceiver and the scholar. The pilot and the tower."

"That's us."

"Together," she said.

The word held everything. The bridge, the confession, the Strahov, the conference, and the distance that was growing.

Together wasn't a solution to the gap. It was a decision to stand on both sides of it at once.

Which was exactly what the Third Way required.
