## PART 3: THE MANUSCRIPT — CORE VISUAL IMAGERY

The following images and spaces are **actually in the book**. Any cover imagery should be grounded in this list, not invented.

### Primary setting / central artifact
**The hyperboloid resonance chamber:**
Seven meters in diameter, five meters tall at the apex. Lower walls are conventional stone rising from a circular floor. At two meters' height, the walls begin curving inward along hyperbolic surfaces — concavities and convexities that function as acoustic focusing surfaces, creating multiple focal points. The apex narrows toward a central point without closing, leaving a small circular oculus — described in the text as "a release point, not a skylight." Warm golden light emanates from within. The chamber resonates at exactly 111.23 Hz — the same frequency as seismic microseisms, the background vibration of the Earth's crust. It is described explicitly as "a cave above ground" — non-Euclidean geometry replicating underground acoustic conditions.

**The eight silver cymatics discs:**
Each 4.1 cm in diameter, each engraved with a different interference pattern: nested circles, overlapping triangles, five concentric rings connected by golden-ratio spirals. They are cross-sections of the three-dimensional chamber specification — stack them in sequence and the chamber geometry assembles. The text describes them as "frozen cymatics experiments — someone vibrated a metal surface at specific frequencies, documented the patterns, and engraved them into silver. In the thirteenth century."

**SubTropolis, Kansas City:**
A 160-foot descent into 270-million-year-old limestone. Pre-Columbian carvings covering every surface. Commercial mining operations above; ancient knowledge below. Blake's experience entering: "standing inside a pattern. Not observing one."

**The Strahov Library, Prague / cave beneath:**
Medieval manuscript vault. The camera praeparationis (preparation chamber) beneath the castle. Where the codex originated.

**The Sagrada Família, Barcelona:**
Gaudí's hyperboloid columns and catenary arches — described in the text as the closest built approximation of the resonance chamber spec. "He wasn't building a church. He was building a cave above ground."

**U-2 spy plane cockpit at 70,000 feet:**
William Masters (Blake's grandfather) as a Cold War U-2 pilot. From the edge of the atmosphere, he could perceive the geometric patterns in the land below — and first understood they were real.

**Seven notebooks:**
Sealed in a safety deposit box for 57 years, timed to open when Blake would be ready. Thirty years of his grandfather's research linking acoustic resonance to the architecture of human thought.

**The black-iron-bound codex:**
The original manuscript, burned by the Church in 1267 after seven copies were scattered across seven cities. "He has been told it was not made by human hands."

### Key thematic images (emotional/abstract, not literal)
- Standing waves made visible in stone — sound as architecture
- The convergence point: "Every frequency path converges here. Every reflection, every harmonic, every overtone. It's not just a focal point, it's a node. Everything arrives. Nothing leaves."
- The gap between Blake and Nadia — love and perception that are structurally incompatible: "the same gift that lets Blake perceive the woman he loves more completely than anyone ever has is the gift that is carrying him beyond her reach"
- Three-generation inheritance — the same handwriting, the same left-handedness, the same gift

### What has been in the trilogy covers already (Books 1–3)
- **Book 1** (*Inheritance of Frequency*): Black background. Gold cymatics mandala — a physical interference pattern rendered as glowing gold lines on black. Wide-tracked Cinzel caps: "MASTERS X" / "THE INHERITANCE OF FREQUENCY" / "VOLUME ONE". Medieval Latin manuscript text ghosted across the background as a watermark. Deeply elegant. This is the established visual identity.
- **Book 2** (*The Grimoire*): Medieval astronomical instrument. Same black ground, same type system.
- **Book 3** (*The Kingdom*): Medieval astrolabe, silver-grey, highly detailed. Same type system.

**The omnibus cover must feel like the culmination of these three — not a fourth volume, but the complete work elevated.**

## PART 4: WHAT WAS TRIED AND FAILED — DO NOT REPEAT

**Failure 1 — The Pantheon dome (Versions 1–9, abandoned months ago)**
A Roman Pantheon interior was proposed because of the oculus/dome connection. This was rejected because: (a) the Pantheon is not in the book, (b) it reads as Dan Brown / Da Vinci Code, (c) it broke continuity with the established black/gold cymatics identity. DO NOT return to Roman dome imagery. It will be rejected immediately.

**Failure 2 — The computational compositor approach (Versions 1–7, this session)**
An attempt was made to composite the jacket programmatically using ReportLab + Python, placing an approved Midjourney hero image (the resonance chamber tunnel, --ar 2:3, warm amber/stone spiral toward golden oculus) over a black ground with vector typography. The approach was technically sound but the agent kept introducing bugs: stretching the hero image across the full spread, using 400% ink registration black instead of K100, failing to fill the bleed, and losing the Real-ESRGAN upscale. The design direction (chamber + Cinzel gold/white on black) was correct; the execution kept failing. v7 (included in this package) is the closest — front panel shows the chamber correctly, typography is approximately right, but it still has: (a) the background at ~RGB 34,31,31 instead of true black, (b) image sharpness still below what the upscale should have produced.

## PART 5: COVER DESIGN BRIEF — START FRESH

### The assignment
Design an omnibus dust jacket that:
1. **Feels like literary fiction at the Tartt/Doerr/Eco level** — not thriller, not genre, not commercial
2. **Is rooted in the actual manuscript imagery** (chamber, discs, limestone, frequency, convergence)
3. **Extends rather than breaks** the Book 1 visual language (black ground, gold, Cinzel, cymatics)
4. **Is iconic** — a cover someone frames, a cover that sells millions

### Recommended creative directions (pick one, develop fully, don't blend)

**Direction A — The Cymatics Mandala Evolved**
The Book 1 cover is a single cymatics interference pattern. The omnibus cover could be the complete system — all eight disc patterns overlaid and resolved into a single unified figure. Not three stacked circles (previous failed attempt) but one complex, deeply detailed interference mandala that contains within it all the nested geometries of the trilogy. Pure black ground, pure gold lines, same Cinzel typography but at omnibus scale. No photography, no architecture — pure mathematical beauty.
*Comp reference: Radiohead album covers, Penguin Classics deluxe editions, Knopf literary hardcovers.*

**Direction B — The Chamber, Done Right**
A photoreal or hyperrealistic rendering of the hyperboloid resonance chamber — not a photograph of an existing building, but a purpose-built image: non-Euclidean stone walls curving toward each other, warm amber/limestone tones, the small oculus at the apex glowing with concentrated golden light, the eight cymatics discs visible on the walls. Shot from inside, looking up. Composition is portrait (--ar 2:3 for Midjourney). Dark zones at top and bottom for text overlay.
*The Midjourney prompt used in v7 sessions produced a usable image — see HERO_IMAGE_GENERATION section below.*

**Direction C — Abstraction: The Convergence Point**
Pure abstract — the moment where all frequency paths converge to the single focal node. Standing waves in amber and gold, concentric and expanding, against black. Neither mandala nor architecture — pure acoustic energy made visible. Closer to contemporary art book design.
*Comp reference: Sean Freeman cover work, Chip Kidd late-career, McSweeney's literary editions.*

**Direction D — The Object**
Close-up, photoreal, hyperdetailed: one of the eight silver cymatics discs, held in a hand (or resting on aged parchment), the engraved interference pattern catching warm raking light. The entire cover occupied by this single object. Elegant and mysterious.
*Comp reference: The Goldfinch hardcover, The Shadow of the Wind, A Discovery of Witches UK edition.*

### Non-negotiables regardless of direction
- **Black background** — the ground of the entire design system
- **Gold and/or warm amber as primary accent color** — must feel warm, precious, ancient
- **Cinzel typeface** for all display text (title, author name) — wide-tracked, inscriptional caps
- **EB Garamond Italic** for all body text (blurbs, bio, flap text)
- **No Dan Brown / thriller register** — no foreshortened architecture, no dramatic lighting that reads as commercial thriller
- **Must feel like the most important book in the trilogy** — the complete work, not a fourth installment

## PART 7: TECHNICAL SPECIFICATIONS (IngramSpark HC Dust Jacket)

### Document dimensions
- **Page size:** 24" × 12.5" (1728 × 900 pts at 72ppi; 7200 × 3750 px at 300 DPI)
- **Format:** PDF/X-1a or PDF/X-3, CMYK only, no RGB, no spot colors
- **Resolution:** 300 DPI minimum for all raster elements

### Panel layout (from template 9798295884412-Jacket.pdf — authoritative)
These coordinates are extracted directly from the IngramSpark template file, not calculated:

| Panel | Left edge | Right edge | Width |
|-------|-----------|------------|-------|
| Back Flap | 0" | 4.985" (358.95 pt) | 4.985" |
| Back Cover | 4.985" | 11.812" (850.5 pt) | 6.827" |
| Spine | 11.812" | 13.437" (967.5 pt) | 1.625" |
| Front Cover | 13.437" | 20.265" (1459.1 pt) | 6.828" |
| Front Flap | 20.265" | 24" (1728 pt) | 3.735" |

- **Bleed:** 0.25" all sides (BleedBox: 115.95, 164.88, 1702.08, 864.0 pts)
- **TrimBox:** 133.95, 182.88, 1684.08, 846.0 pts
- **Safety margin for text:** 0.5" from all fold lines and trim edges

### Color
- **Background black:** CMYK (0, 0, 0, 1) — K-only, 100% total ink. NOT registration black (1,1,1,1) which is 400% ink and will fail preflight
- **Gold:** CMYK approximately (0, 0.15, 0.60, 0.25) — warm, not cool
- **Cream body text:** CMYK (0, 0, 0.05, 0.10)
- **Maximum total ink coverage:** 240% (IngramSpark limit)
- **ICC Profile:** FOGRA39L_coated.icc — must be embedded; all image conversion must use this profile via PIL ImageCms, not naive Pillow .convert('CMYK')

### Typography
- **Display font:** Cinzel (Trajan-class inscriptional caps)
  - Title "MASTERS X": ~36pt, 500 tracking (0.5em letter-spacing), white
  - Subtitle "THE COMPLETE TRILOGY": ~18pt, 350 tracking, gold
  - Author name: ~14pt, 400 tracking, white
  - Headers (ABOUT THE AUTHOR, etc.): ~10pt, 350 tracking, gold
- **Body font:** EB Garamond Italic
  - Synopsis/flap body: ~11pt, 15pt leading
  - Bio: ~10pt, 14pt leading
  - All body text: cream/off-white, not pure white
- Letter-spacing on all Cinzel must match Book 1 reference — letters breathe, not compressed

### Barcode
- EAN-13 for ISBN 9798295884412
- White background box, approximately 2.0" × 1.2"
- Position: bottom-right of back cover panel
- Clearance: 0.75" from bottom trim, 0.5" minimum from spine fold line

### Spine text
- Three elements distributed evenly top-to-bottom, each as a single rotated text string (90° CW)
- NOT individual stacked letters

### PDF metadata
- Set TrimBox, BleedBox, and MediaBox explicitly and distinctly
- All text must be vector (live text objects), not rasterized

## PART 12: MODEL RECOMMENDATION

For the creative direction and design work: **Use a human designer.** Seriously. The computational approach has been attempted 7+ times. The typography and layout are achievable via Python/ReportLab, but the *creative* problem — what image, what composition, what feeling — needs a human art director or professional book cover designer who can look at the comps, read the manuscript passages in Part 3, and make aesthetic judgments that a script cannot.

**Recommended path:**
1. Hire a professional book cover designer via Reedsy (reedsy.com/designers) — filter for literary fiction, look at portfolios, budget $500–1500 for a full HC jacket
2. OR: Use this handoff document to brief a human designer on Upwork or 99designs
3. OR: If continuing with AI — use **Claude Opus** or **GPT-4o** with vision to evaluate mockups against the Book 1 reference, and a separate dedicated compositor agent for technical execution — do not conflate creative judgment and technical execution in the same agent loop

**If you must continue with computational approach:**
The single most important change: **generate a PNG mockup first, get approval, then generate the PDF.** Every failure in the v1–v7 series came from jumping straight to PDF without visual confirmation of the design. A PNG takes seconds to generate and review. A bad PDF takes 20 iterations to fix.
