Chapter Seven. The Deeper Mystery.

One hundred nine hertz. Kansas City.


Morning Coffee #2 was different.

Blake knew it was different before he opened his eyes. 3:47 again. The same internal alarm. But this time, when he got up, his mind was already in the notebooks. Already tracing connections between the 1882 Wyandotte cave article and the sealed entrance notation on the USGS geological survey.

Nadia was awake on the couch. She'd stayed again. The third night in a row, each time with the same clinical framing (someone should be here while you're processing), each time with the same unspoken current underneath.

"Coffee?" Blake said from the kitchen.

"Please."

He made two cups. Set one on the arm of the couch and turned back toward his laptop before Nadia had finished sitting up, before any of the small morning rituals that had felt, three days ago, like the beginning of something.

"You found something," she said.

"Kansas City sits on caves. I found an 1882 newspaper article. A reporter lowered into a well in Wyandotte discovered an underground river three miles from SubTropolis."

"Blake -"

"SubTropolis wasn't built. It was discovered. The Bethany Falls Limestone Company found existing cave systems in the 1940s. The caves Moreau used were there decades before. Centuries before."

"Blake."

He stopped. Turned. Nadia was sitting on the couch with her coffee untouched, watching him with concern. Patience. And underneath both, the beginning of loss.

"You haven't looked at me since I woke up," she said.

The sentence landed. She was right. He'd been talking at the laptop, at the research. He'd been talking past her as you talk past furniture.

"I'm sorry," he said.

"Don't be sorry. Just tell me about the caves. But tell me, not the screen."

He told her. Slower this time. Watching her face as her scholar's mind assembled context around each new piece of evidence. When he mentioned the USGS notation, Cave entrance (sealed), historical marker removed 1967. Her expression sharpened.

"Removed," she said. "Not relocated."

"Someone erased it deliberately."

"The Custodians clean up after themselves." She turned to the window. The gray sedan was there. "1967 would be right. The commercial operation was expanding."

"We need to find Moreau's journal," she said. "A personal journal, his record of where he hid the manuscripts."

"I think I know where it is. 1112 Pennsylvania Avenue. Moreau's last known address."



They took Nadia's car. She drove with quiet competence. Checking mirrors, varying routes, making two unnecessary turns to confirm the gray sedan was following.

Blake sat in the passenger seat watching the morning city. His perception was sharper than yesterday. Power lines creating interference patterns against the October sky.

"You're doing it right now," Nadia said.

"Doing what?"

"Going somewhere I can't follow." She said it without accusation. "Your eyes change when the perception takes over. You're seeing something different. Something more."

"Does it bother you?"

"It scares me. Not the perception itself. How fast it's progressing." A pause. "How far away you're getting."

They ordered at the counter. Blake's coffee habits, inherited from his father, were becoming daily rituals he shared with Nadia now. They sat near the back, both facing the door.

The interior was exposed brick and reclaimed wood.

Except.

Blake's eyes tracked along the brick wall. A section near the back corner where the mortar color didn't match. Bricks set at a slightly different angle.

"That wall," he said.

"What am I not seeing?"

"It was rebuilt. The rest is original 1889. That section isn't." He walked to the wall. Up close, the difference was obvious to him. And a pattern in the protruding bricks. A Fibonacci spiral.

The third brick from the center moved.

A soft click. The wall section swung inward, just enough to reveal a dark cavity. Inside, wrapped in oilcloth that crackled with age, a journal.

Blake pulled it out carefully. Opened it. The pages were filled with geometric patterns and encrypted text.

But when Blake relaxed his focus, the symbols resolved.

Father Vincent Moreau. Personal journal. June nineteen twenty. June nineteen twenty-four.

"Can you read it?" Nadia's voice was tight.

"Yes. The encryption resolves when I focus on it. Like a lock that only opens for a specific key."

"Moreau designed it that way. If you can read it, you're one of us. If you can't -" She stopped. Swallowed. "It's just gibberish."

Blake heard what she didn't say. The journal was in her tradition, part of the knowledge she'd devoted her life to, and she couldn't read it.

"Tell me what the dates mean," he said. "The historical context. You can read that better than I can."

It was a small thing. An invitation. Come into this through the door you can open.

They read together. Blake translating, Nadia supplying the framework. The journal documented manuscript locations, access routes through cave systems Moreau had mapped in detail.

And a final entry. June 4, 1924. Two days before Moreau drowned.

I cannot know if I serve truth or if truth has abandoned me to madness. I can only document what I see and pray that those who come after can determine which I have become.

Nadia's hand found his forearm. A grip. As if she were holding onto something she could feel slipping.

"We need to go," Blake said. "If I can find this, someone else can too."



They were halfway to the car when Blake saw the priest.

Father Crane stood on the corner. Black suit, white collar. He walked toward them with purpose.

"Mr. Masters. We need to talk."

"I don't think we do."

"I think we do." Crane fell into step beside them. His eyes moved to Nadia. "Sister Volkov. You're keeping interesting company."

Nadia said nothing. Her hand on her bag strap, knuckles white.

"You've been busy," Crane said. "Newspaper archives. Your grandfather's notebooks. A late-night visit to a coffee shop with hidden compartments." A thin smile. "Did you find what you were looking for?"

"You've been following us."

"Observing. There's a difference." Crane stopped. Turned. "I know what's happening to you. The way the world has started to reveal itself. The Church has centuries of experience."

"Is that what you did to Father Moreau? Protected him?"

Pain flickered across Crane's face. "Moreau made dangerous choices. The result was unfortunate."

"He was murdered."

"He was stopped." Crane's eyes were steady. "The patterns you're seeing. They're beautiful. Seductive. But they don't tell you the cost."

"And you decide who's ready?"

"Someone has to." Crane reached into his pocket. A card. White cardstock, embossed cross, phone number. "The Church can help you control what you're experiencing."

Blake studied the card. Looked at Nadia, who was standing very still with an expression Blake had never seen, not fear, not anger. Recognition.

"Your father didn't accept our help either," Crane said. "Think about what happened to the one who didn't stay quiet."

He walked away. Nadia exhaled, a long, controlled breath.

"You know him," Blake said.

"He killed my mentor. Sister Katarina. In Prague. 2019. Made it look like a stroke." She took the card from Blake's hand, tore it in half. "That's what the Custodians' help looks like."

They walked to the car in silence. This silence had edges. The shape of what Nadia had just revealed. That she wasn't only a scholar. She was a person who'd lost someone to the same war that had taken Blake's father.

The grief was structural, Blake realized. Not something that had happened to her. Something she was built from.

Like his.



Back at the apartment, they mapped the underground network from Moreau's journal. Andrew arrived at noon. By evening, they had a plan. SubTropolis. The deep chambers.

Andrew left at nine. Nadia stayed.

"He's right about one thing," Blake said. "Crane. The perception is accelerating. I can feel it. Like a radio dial turning, picking up more stations."

"Your grandfather's exercises. Notebook Six." Nadia pulled the notebook from her bag. "We should have started days ago."

"Why didn't we?"

She paused. "Because you were moving so fast. Finding things. And I didn't want to be the person who slowed you down."

"You're not slowing me down," he said.

"I know." She opened Notebook Six. "But I'm about to. These exercises take time. Patience. You'll hate them."

"Probably."

"Definitely." The smile of a person who'd decided to do something difficult because it was necessary. "We start tonight. And Blake. The exercises work better with a partner. Someone to anchor you. So I'll be here. Every night. Until you can control it."

I'll be here.

"Okay," Blake said.

Nadia opened the notebook. Drew her chair closer. Their knees touched under the table.

"Close your eyes," she said. "And listen."

Blake closed his eyes. Nadia's voice began. Steady, precise, guiding him through the first exercise William had documented thirty years ago.

Outside, the gray sedan watched.

Inside, Nadia's voice held Blake steady while his brain learned to fly.
