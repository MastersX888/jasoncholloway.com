### BEAT 1 — The record (0:40–2:30)

> The facts, graded *documented*: radiocarbon dating at the University of Arizona, 2009, put the vellum at roughly 1404 to 1438. The earliest clear reference is a 1666 letter from Georg Baresch, a Prague alchemist who owned it and couldn't read it either. He sent pages to Athanasius Kircher in Rome. Kircher never cracked it.
>
> And the letter chain records something else: Emperor Rudolf II of Bohemia paid six hundred gold ducats for this book. That was serious money. Rudolf thought it mattered.

[OST: `1404–1438 · 600 ducats`]

---

### BEAT 2 — Why it resists (2:30–4:15)

> The strangeness is statistical. The text obeys Zipf's law — the word-frequency curve of natural languages. That rules out simple randomness. But no key has ever produced consistent translations. Three possibilities stand, none ruled out: an unknown or invented language; a hoax sophisticated enough to fake linguistic structure; or a cipher we haven't identified.
>
> The botanical drawings sharpen it. Many of the plants match no identified species. They aren't heraldic symbols. They look like biological illustrations — of something.

[OST: `Zipf's law — passes · translation — none`]
