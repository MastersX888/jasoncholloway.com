## Manual deletion on Facebook (when Outstand cannot remove live posts)

Outstand can delete scheduled or queued posts in its dashboard, but **posts already published to Facebook may remain visible on the Page** even after Outstand removes its record. If you do not see a delete option in the regular Facebook app, you are likely viewing the Page as yourself rather than as Page admin.

**Switch to Page admin first**

1. Open [Meta Business Suite](https://business.facebook.com/) (or the Facebook mobile app).
2. Use the profile menu (top right) and choose **Switch Profile** (or **See all profiles**).
3. Select the **Seventh City Press** Page, not your personal profile.
4. Confirm the interface shows you are acting as the Page (Page name appears in the header).

**Delete a Page post in Meta Business Suite**

1. In Business Suite, go to **Content** → **Posts & stories** (or **Planner** → **Posts**).
2. Filter to the **Seventh City Press** Facebook Page if prompted.
3. Find the post (essay-mirror copy with author research voice is the wrong batch; catalog posts replace those).
4. Open the post menu (**⋯**) → **Delete post** → confirm.

**Delete from the Facebook Page directly (desktop)**

1. Switch to the Page profile as above.
2. Open the Page’s **Posts** tab.
3. Click **⋯** on the post → **Delete** → confirm.

If **Delete** still does not appear, check **Page roles** under Page Settings: your account needs **Admin** or **Editor** access on the Seventh City Press Page.

## Post 2 · Volume I — *The Inheritance of Frequency*

**Headline:** Vol. I: Seven notebooks. Thirty years of research. One box that opens on schedule.

**Body:**

**Masters X: The Inheritance of Frequency** opens the trilogy beneath Kansas City.

Blake Masters loses his job guarding the subterranean limestone vaults of SubTropolis after photographing geometric carvings in a tunnel no official map acknowledges. A safety deposit box his grandfather paid for fifty-seven years in advance arrives on the day Blake is finally ready to open it. Inside: seven notebooks of classified acoustic research and a cross-reference to a crypt sealed beneath the Strahov Library in Prague since the thirteenth century.

Volume I introduces the Distribution File, the 111.2 Hz signature that runs through the fiction (distinct from measured archaeoacoustic literature), and Sister Nadia Volkov, whose dissertation treats grimoires as cognitive technologies long before the plot proves her right.

Trade paperback and hardcover ship through IngramSpark to bookstores and libraries worldwide. Kindle edition available on Amazon.

## Post 4 · Volume III — *The Kingdom*

**Headline:** Vol. III: The demonstration, the argument, and what open release costs.

**Body:**

**Masters X: The Kingdom** closes the trilogy in Kansas City.

Blake returns with a restructured nervous system and a preparation protocol that can expand human consciousness safely, but Father Crane’s published theological critique forces a question the Foundation cannot dodge: if the frequency is what the tradition described as the kingdom within, is every week of preparation gatekeeping prayer?

Twenty-three candidates. Seventy-two hours. A limestone chamber where the frequency has resonated since before the city was built. Four hundred twelve listening sites in sixty-one countries. And an ending that chooses documentation over confrontation: a file released under CC0, the license that reserves nothing, not even attribution.

Volume III is where the series’ argument about classification, leakage, and public science becomes explicit. Seventh City Press recommends pairing the novel with the author’s Field Notes and blog essays for readers who want the fiction labeled as fiction and the sources cited as sources.

## Post 6 · Monograph — scholarly list (Post 1 of 2)

**Headline:** Beyond the trilogy: literary criticism from Seventh City Press.

**Body:**

The Seventh City Press scholarly list opens with **Innocence, Desire, and the Architecture of the Fall: The Grape and Its Counter-Symbols in the Fiction of John Hawkes** by Jason Carroll Holloway.

Originally completed as a graduate thesis at Mercy University, this monograph is the first comprehensive motif analysis across John Hawkes’s complete seventeen-novel corpus. Holloway traces the word “grape” through Hawkes’s mature fiction (129 documented occurrences) and maps its counter-symbols: the Camera, the Wounded Body, the Architecture of Enclosure, and the Child. The argument is structural: Hawkes built fiction from spatial and symbolic logic, not plot alone.

The monograph is available in trade paperback, hardcover, and EPUB on Google Play Books.

## Post 7 · Monograph — the grape motif (Post 2 of 2)

**Headline:** 129 grapes. Seventeen novels. One architecture.

**Body:**

Why include a John Hawkes monograph on a fiction publisher's list?

Because the monograph demonstrates the method the trilogy applies in fiction: count what repeats, map where it clusters, name the gap between what the archive shows and what the novelist invents.

The monograph’s central claim is quantitative before it is interpretive. The grape appears 129 times across Hawkes’s fiction, concentrated where innocence, desire, and enclosure converge. Holloway reads that recurrence as deliberate literary engineering beneath apparent chaos, the same discipline the Masters X novels apply to frequency, manuscript tradition, and Kansas City geography.

Readers of the trilogy who want Holloway’s scholarly voice without the conspiracy plot will find it here. Review copies available to accredited critics through the imprint communications desk.

**Link:** https://jasoncholloway.com/books/hawkes-monograph/

## Post 8 · Field Notes and the Analysis Chamber

**Headline:** The research layer: Field Notes and the Analysis Chamber.

**Body:**

Seventh City Press publishes fiction that cites its sources. The **Field Notes** on jasoncholloway.com are the open research layer behind the Masters X Trilogy: archaeoacoustics and 111 Hz, cymatics, Kansas City locations and SubTropolis, the Voynich Manuscript, the Ars Notoria, declassified translation files, and more.

Each note distinguishes measured claims from invented ones. Where no published study exists (for example, acoustics of the Kansas City underground), the Field Notes say so. 

The **Analysis Chamber** goes further: an interactive research archive where readers can run the same measurements the characters run in the novels. Seventh City Press treats that research layer as part of the catalog.

Start with the Field Notes hub, then follow links from any Masters X volume page.
