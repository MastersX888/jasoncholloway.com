## 2. The two prose rewrites, verbatim

### Andrew Tanaka → Nolan Eriksen (Book 3)

The `the other Andrew` appositive existed only to disambiguate him from Andrew
Chen. With the collision gone the repair work is pointless, so the clause was
rewritten rather than substituted.

**Before**

> …All six surfaces are within 0.3% of the Specchi specification. The Iceland
> basalt chamber is stable at 111.2. **Andrew, the other Andrew, Tanaka,**
> confirmed the satellite uplink from Reykjavik. The panel is arriving at seven
> AM.…

**After**

> …All six surfaces are within 0.3% of the Specchi specification. The Iceland
> basalt chamber is stable at 111.2. **Nolan Eriksen** confirmed the satellite
> uplink from Reykjavik. The panel is arriving at seven AM.…

−20 characters. Surrounding punctuation and the list rhythm of Nadia's
status recital are untouched. He also collided with **Yuki Tanaka**, so this one
edit clears two collisions.

### Sabrina Volkov — in-law → sister (Book 2)

**Before**

> Nadia opened a new email. Recipient: Sabrina Volkov, M.D., the clinical
> director **whose last name Nadia shared by marriage** and whose clinical
> assessments had the compassion of a sister and the precision of a specialist in
> neurological monitoring who happened to know that the subject in question was
> also her brother-in-law.

**After**

> Nadia opened a new email. Recipient: Sabrina Volkov, M.D., the clinical
> director **whose last name Nadia had been born with** and whose clinical
> assessments had the compassion of a sister and the precision of a specialist in
> neurological monitoring who happened to know that the subject in question was
> also her brother-in-law.

`shared by marriage` → `had been born with`. **Exactly 18 characters either way,
so the edit is byte-neutral and cannot reflow or repaginate anything.**

Three notes on why this phrasing rather than "who was also her sister":

1. **The sentence already says "sister" downstream** ("the compassion of a
   sister"). Putting "sister" in the relative clause too would repeat it inside
   one sentence and flatten the reveal. The chosen wording keeps the sentence's
   own later use of "sister" as the payoff.
2. **It keeps the observation the sentence was actually making** — that the two
   women share a surname — and merely corrects *how*. Sisters share it by birth;
   Volkov is Nadia's pre-marriage name (she practises the signature "Nadia
   Masters" in Book 1), so "had been born with" is the precise fact.
3. **"her brother-in-law" at the end is correct under the sister reading and was
   left alone.** Nadia and Sabrina are sisters, Nadia married Blake, so Blake is
   Sabrina's brother-in-law. Nothing else in the sentence needed to move.

**Discrepancy against the brief, for the record.** The task described this
sentence as ending "the compassion of a woman and the precision of an
instrument." That is a *different* Sabrina sentence — Book 2, Chapter One:
"Permanent, according to Sabrina Volkov, whose clinical assessments had the
compassion of a woman and the precision of an instrument." That earlier sentence
makes **no relationship claim at all** and needed no edit. The sentence carrying
the "by marriage" error is the email-recipient sentence quoted above, and it was
already using "sister" and "brother-in-law" — which is further evidence the
author's sister ruling is the right one.
