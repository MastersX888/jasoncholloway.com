Chapter Nineteen. Return.

One hundred nine hertz. Kansas City.


The apartment had been entered while they were in Prague.

Blake knew before he opened the door. Dust patterns disturbed, electromagnetic signature of a place methodically searched. His enhanced senses caught subtleties: nitrile glove residue on the door handle, the lock picked rather than forced.

"Wait," he said. Nadia stopped behind him. He opened the door.

Not ransacked. Precise. Every drawer searched and carefully replaced. The fireproof safe defeated by tools that left no marks. They wanted him to know. We can reach you.

They'd taken the notebooks. The copies. His laptop. Everything related to the research.

But they'd left everything else. The Breitling on the nightstand. William's model aircraft. His clothes, furniture. Untouched. The selectivity was its own message. They weren't interested in hurting him. They were interested in the knowledge.

And on the counter, Sarah's wine bottle. The Grüner Veltliner she'd brought the last week they were together. The Watchers hadn't touched it. He picked it up. Even grief came to him as data now. He set it down.

"Professional," Nadia said from the doorway. "Entry pattern matches what Miroslav described. Watchers."

"The SD card?"

"Bus station locker," Blake said. "Cash, fake name. William's paranoid redundancy."

"Then we still have the digital copies."

"We have copies. But the notebooks were originals. Without them, the provenance chain breaks."

Nadia stopped at the kitchen table. "Blake. The table."

He looked. The Watchers had taken his laptop from the surface. But underneath. Taped to the underside. A manila envelope. James's unfinished manuscript. The Watchers had searched the table and missed what was underneath.

William's principle. Hide things in the obvious places.

"They were looking for research materials," Blake said. "This looks like personal papers."

"Or they didn't care about fiction."

Blake almost smiled. "That's what my father was counting on."



They moved that evening. Andrew's grandmother's house in Gladstone. Paid off, no digital connection to Blake.

Blake packed fast. Clothes. The Breitling. William's photograph. James's keys. The manuscript.

And the wine bottle. He didn't know why he packed it. It was a bottle a woman had brought to a relationship that didn't survive. Blake put it in his bag as you keep a photograph of a house you no longer live in.

Nadia watched him pack the bottle. Said nothing.

Andrew had security running by midnight. The next morning, Blake retrieved the SD card. Everything William had documented. Everything Blake had photographed.

The originals were gone. But the knowledge survived.

Redundancy defeats suppression.Redundancy defeats suppression.

That first Gladstone morning, Blake made coffee. Two cups. Nadia's first.

Morning Coffee #4.

He brought it to the table where she was working. Latin transcriptions spread around her laptop. She took the cup without looking up. Both hands.

It cost him something. Each time, a little more.

"You're doing the thing," Nadia said without glancing up.

"What thing?"

"The choosing thing. Closing the aperture to see me instead of the math." She set down her pen. "I can tell because your jaw tightens."

"It's getting harder."

"I know." She picked up her coffee. "The Strahov accelerated everything. Your perception is running at a level it wasn't designed for."

"How long before I can't close the aperture at all?"

She let the silence lengthen.

"I don't know. Months. Maybe less." She paused. "Blake. We need to talk about what happens when -"

"Not yet." He took her hand. "Not this morning. This morning we drink coffee and you work on the Latin and I don't look at the math in your face."

"Okay. This morning."

But they both heard it, the qualifier. This morning. Not every morning.

Morning Coffee #4. The last one that felt like the first ones.

They drank it slowly.
