Chapter Sixteen. The Keepers Revealed.

One eleven point two hertz. Prague.


The evening after the Strahov, Blake couldn't look at Nadia without seeing the geometry.

She'd ordered water. Not wine. Water. The retreat into clarity, into the version of herself that could sit across from a man whose perception was dissolving her face into mathematics. Blake noticed the choice as he noticed everything now: tea was patience, coffee was work, wine was honesty, and water was the sound a person made when they were pulling back behind the last wall they had.

She stopped mid-sentence. "What?"

"Nothing."

"You're doing it again. The looking-through-me thing."

Blake closed the aperture another notch. The geometry receded. Reluctantly. And revealed the face underneath. Nadia. Dark eyes, concern.

"Sorry. The chamber, it's taking longer to fade than I expected."

"Is it fading?"

"No."

Nadia set down her fork. "What do you see? When you look at me now."

"I see you. But I also see. The mathematics of you. The proportional relationships." He paused. "It's like someone turned up the resolution. The image is the same. But there's more information in every pixel."

"And the information is more interesting than the image."

Blake didn't answer. Which was answer enough.

"Miroslav wants to see us tomorrow," she said. "The political surface, it's more complicated than we knew."

"What's the complication?"

"There's a fourth faction. The Watchers."



Miroslav had laid out documents. Photographs, letters, organizational charts. Sixty years of accumulated intelligence.

"The Watchers predate the Custodians by centuries," Miroslav said. "They don't suppress or preserve the knowledge. They observe. Monitor. Track bloodlines, including yours."

He handed Blake a letter in William's handwriting. April nineteen sixty-eight.

They approached me at the Northrop facility. Two men. Government credentials, but not government agents. They knew about Prague. Their offer: join their observation network. In exchange, complete protection for my family.

I declined. James is three years old.

"The Watchers have been monitoring the Masters bloodline since at least 1880," Miroslav said. "They don't act. They watch. And when someone gets too close -"

"They arrange accidents."

"Your father. 2010. Five months after finding William's notebooks."

Blake set the letter down. "How many factions total?"

"Four that matter. Custodians, suppression. Completion Sect. Forced evolution. Watchers. Observation and control. They killed your father." Miroslav paused. "And Keepers. Us. Preservation and voluntary awakening."

"There's a fifth option. The Third Way."

"Rudolf's demonstration. Understanding affecting water at a measurable scale. Documented, verified, replicated. Making the knowledge so public that all four factions become irrelevant." Miroslav pulled out a thick folder. "Enhanced individuals at seven geographic locations. Coordinated activation over 72 hours. Independent scientific verification at each site. The methodology is four centuries old, but the measurement technology now exists."

"One of the sites is in Missouri," Blake said.

"Yes. And Prague. Venice. Alexandria. Beijing. Cusco. And a seventh. Underwater. Location uncertain. Rudolf's scholars called it 'Site Echo.'"

"The demonstration would require going public. Completely."

"Yes. Which means every faction responds." Miroslav met Blake's eyes. "And you stand in the center and offer the knowledge freely. Not to any faction. To everyone."

"If I do this, what happens to the people around me?"

Miroslav was quiet.

"The Watchers killed my father. If I go public, every person I care about becomes a target."

"Your grandfather asked the same question. In this same room." The old man's voice was gentle. "Silence doesn't protect them anymore, Blake. It only protects the knowledge. And the knowledge doesn't need protection, it needs distribution."

"The conference," Blake said. "In two days. All the factions in one place."

"The Keeper network has arranged a panel. Medieval manuscript analysis. Legitimate academic context. You could use it to make the first move. Show them what you can do. Make them understand the knowledge is already distributed."

Blake turned to Nadia. "What do you think?"

"I think you should let me present the historical framework while you demonstrate the perceptual capabilities. Together."

Two ways of reading. Scholar and perceiver.

"Together," Blake agreed.

"But Blake," Miroslav said, "be prepared for what happens after. Once you demonstrate publicly, there's no going back."

"My grandfather was willing to die in silence. My father was willing to die trying. I'd rather die demonstrating."

Miroslav looked at Nadia. Then back at Blake.

"Your grandfather came alone. Your father investigated alone. But you brought someone worth fighting for. That's the difference."

Blake and Nadia stood in the space between the window and the door. She took his hand.

"The restaurant last night," she said. "The geometry in my face."

"Nadia -"

"When you closed the aperture, when you stopped seeing the math and started seeing ME. I could tell. I could see the moment you came back." Her grip tightened. "Keep coming back. That's all I'm asking."

"I will."

"And when you can't anymore, tell me. Don't pretend."

"I won't."

She released his hand. Became the operative again.

"Two days," she said. "Let's make them count."
