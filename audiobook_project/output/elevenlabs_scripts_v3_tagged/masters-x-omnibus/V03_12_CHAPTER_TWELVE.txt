Chapter Twelve. Barabbas.

One hundred nine hertz. Kansas City.

[short pause]

The DOD representative wore a suit that cost more than Nadia's monthly salary and sat in the Foundation's conference room with the particular comfort of a man who sat in conference rooms the way other people sat in their living rooms, habitually, without effort, with the profound ease of someone who had learned that the most effective way to take something was to sit still while the thing came to you.

His name was Colonel David Reese. His card said Department of Defense, Science and Technology Directorate. His handshake was dry and his eye contact was engineered, calibrated, Nadia noted, to convey trustworthiness while simultaneously communicating that trustworthiness was not the relevant variable.

"We're here to offer protection," Reese said.

"Protection from what?"

"From the institutional opposition you've been experiencing. The patent claims. The FDA inquiry. The IRB challenges. The Lancet editorial. We're aware of all of it, Ms. Volkov. We've been monitoring your situation."

"Monitoring."

"Observing. With interest. The Department has been following Doctor Masters' research since the Strahov crypt. We believe the Foundation's work has significant applications in areas the Department is actively developing."

Nadia poured him coffee. The gesture was automatic, the hostess reflex, the institutional hospitality that she provided to every visitor regardless of whether the visitor was offering something valuable or something dangerous. She poured the coffee into the Foundation's standard white mug and set it in front of him and sat down and folded her hands on the table in the posture that Blake called "the desk" and that was, in fact, the posture of a woman who was about to say no and wanted to say it from a position of architectural stability.

"What are you proposing?"

"Classified status. The Foundation's research would be designated as a classified defense program. You would receive full funding, significantly more than the NIH grant. Full security clearance for your staff. Protection from patent challenges, FDA inquiries, and institutional opposition. In exchange, publication rights would be restricted to pre-approved channels, international collaborations would require review, and the Ghana field site would be suspended pending security assessment."

The room was quiet. The HVAC hummed. Somewhere in the building, Marcus was teaching a class, and twelve students were breathing, and the one hundred eight hertz composite was forming in a fluorescent-lit classroom, and the frequency did not know and did not care that a man in a $2,400 suit was sitting in a conference room proposing to classify it.

Nadia called a board meeting. Thirty minutes. Emergency session.

She presented the offer without commentary. The board members listened. Margaret Chen took notes. Gerald Stern took different notes. Andrew, participating via satellite from Iceland, his face on the conference room screen, the stone cottage visible behind him, the monitoring equipment humming its seven point eight three hertz baseline, said nothing.

Nadia let the silence build. The silence that she had been building in boardrooms for a decade, the silence that was not the absence of decision but the presence of deliberation, the institutional equivalent of the three-minute breathing exercise, the space in which the right answer could emerge because nobody was rushing it.

Then she spoke.

"If we accept classified status, the knowledge survives inside a system that controls its distribution. The DOD decides who hears the frequency. The DOD decides who receives the preparation. The DOD becomes the keeper, and the keeper's mandate is not preservation, it's deployment. That is not what the ninth page says. That is not what any of it says."

She looked around the table.

"The vote is whether to accept. My vote is no."

Margaret: "No."

Gerald: "Abstain." Attorneys always abstained on matters of conscience.

Andrew, from the screen: "No."

Unanimous, minus abstention.

Nadia turned to the screen where Colonel Reese was supposed to be watching via the secure video link she had offered as a courtesy. The screen was dark. He had disconnected five minutes ago. He already knew the answer.

[pause]

Two weeks later, the DOD reclassified the NIH grant as DURC, Dual Use Research of Concern. The reclassification did not stop the research. It added reporting requirements: quarterly disclosures, material transfer agreements, publication review. The institutional equivalent of a leash, not a cage, a leash. You could go where you wanted. You just had to tell them where you were going.

"They didn't arrest us," Nadia said to Blake, that evening, at the kitchen table, the Quality Hill apartment, two cups of coffee and the ninth page between them. "They audited us. That's worse. An arrest is dramatic. An arrest creates martyrs. An audit creates paperwork. Paperwork doesn't inspire anyone. Paperwork just exhausts you."

Blake looked at the ninth page. The two figures, breathing. The open rings.

"The frequency doesn't care about paperwork."

"The frequency doesn't. The institution does. And I am the institution."

She picked up her coffee. The cup was cold. She drank it anyway. Nadia Volkov did not waste coffee, cold or otherwise, the way she did not waste time or words or the institutional energy that she had spent a decade building and that the DOD was now attempting to drain through the slow hemorrhage of compliance requirements.

"I'll handle the paperwork," she said. "You handle the frequency."

"Deal."

"It's always been the deal."

She set down the cup. The kitchen was dark except for the light above the stove, the small light, the intimate light, the light of conversations that happen at the end of the day when the institution is closed and the people who run the institution are just people, sitting at a table, drinking cold coffee, holding the frequency between them the way the ninth page held the two figures: face to face, breathing, the gap between them producing the only interference pattern that mattered.
