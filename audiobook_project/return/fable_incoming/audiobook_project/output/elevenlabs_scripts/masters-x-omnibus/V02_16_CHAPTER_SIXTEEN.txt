Chapter Sixteen. Senator Holt.

One hundred nine hertz. Kansas City.


The Senate hearing room smelled like carpet cleaner and coffee that had been made four hours ago and not replaced because congressional staffers operated on a different metabolic schedule than the rest of the federal government, a schedule calibrated to eighteen-hour days, reheated liquids, and the particular form of insomnia that comes from knowing things the public does not know and will eventually learn and will blame you for not having said sooner.

Nadia wore the blazer. The charcoal one. The institutional armor.

She had arrived forty minutes early because Nadia Volkov arriving early was Nadia Volkov arriving on time, and arriving on time was arriving too late, and the difference between those categories was the difference between controlling the room and being controlled by it, a difference she had learned in the orphanage, where the children who arrived at meals first ate the warm food and the children who arrived second ate testimony to what the warm food had been.

The hearing room was Dirksen 106, a room she had been in twice before, both times as an observer, both times in the gallery seats behind the witness table, both times watching other people explain their work to senators who understood budgets better than research and framed their questions accordingly. This time she was at the table. The microphone was fourteen inches tall, silver, with a foam windscreen that someone had replaced recently, the foam still firm, still black, not yet compressed by the accumulation of testimony that older windscreens displayed as a chronicle of mouths.

Senator Margaret Holt. Chair of the Commerce Committee's Subcommittee on Science, Oceans, Fisheries, and Weather. Seventy-one years old. Methodist. Former prosecutor. The kind of senator who asked questions she already knew the answer to and used the witness's response to establish not the answer but the witness's relationship to the truth, whether they bent toward it or away from it, and by how much, and whether the bending was structural or cosmetic.

"Ms. Volkov. You are the institutional administrator of the Masters Frequency Foundation."

"I am."

"And the Foundation conducts research into acoustic resonance effects on human neurological function."

"We do."

"And this research involves placing human subjects inside stone chambers and exposing them to specific sound frequencies for extended periods of time."

Nadia paused. Not because she didn't have an answer. Because the pause was the answer, the institutional breath that communicated: I see what you are doing. You are framing the work as if it were dangerous. I will now reframe it as if it were careful, which it is, and the reframing will be more precise than your framing because I have been doing this for seven years and you have been reading the briefing packet since Tuesday.

"We conduct a graduated preparation protocol that includes, among other elements, controlled acoustic exposure in geologically appropriate chambers. The protocol is fifty-two weeks long. The acoustic exposure component represents approximately eleven percent of the total preparation sequence. The remainder consists of psychological screening, physical conditioning, informed consent procedures, and ongoing neurological monitoring. I am happy to walk the committee through the full protocol."

"That won't be necessary." Holt looked at her papers. The look was theatrical. Holt knew the papers by heart. The look was for the C-SPAN camera, and the C-SPAN camera was for the public, and the public was for the political record that Holt was constructing with the methodical patience of a woman who had been constructing political records since before Nadia was born.

"What I am interested in, Ms. Volkov, is the Maryland facility. The Cognigenics activation. Your Foundation's protocol was the basis for their work. Your research was cited in their internal documents. And their subjects suffered seizures."

"Senator, I'd like to clarify two things. First: our protocol was not the basis for the Cognigenics program. Our published research was cited by Cognigenics, yes. But citation is not collaboration. Cognigenics cited our work the way any researcher cites published literature, as a reference point. They then deviated from our methodology in every material respect." She paused. "Second: the seizures occurred because Cognigenics compressed a fifty-two-week preparation protocol into six weeks. The preparation is not optional. The preparation is the protection."

"You're saying the difference between your program and the Cognigenics program is time."

"I'm saying the difference is preparation. Senator, are you familiar with the Book of Daniel? Chapter three. The fiery furnace."

Holt's face shifted, the shift of a Methodist who had not expected the Bible in a Commerce Committee hearing. "I am."

"Three men were thrown into a furnace. They walked out unharmed. The text does not present this as magic. It presents it as preparation, they were prepared, by their faith and by their practice, to withstand what the fire was. The fire was the same fire that killed the soldiers who threw them in. Same fire. Same furnace. Different preparation." Nadia leaned forward, exactly two inches, the distance she had calibrated through seven years of institutional confrontation as the maximum lean that communicated urgency without communicating desperation. "What Cognigenics did was throw unprepared people into a furnace. What we do is spend fifty-two weeks teaching them to walk through it."

The room was silent. Holt's staffer, a woman in her early thirties with the specific pallor of someone who spent twelve hours a day in a building that was designed to be impressive rather than habitable, was writing furiously.

"Ms. Volkov. If this technology, and I'm choosing my words carefully, if this technology can harm people when used without preparation, then it can be used as a weapon."

"Anything that affects the human body can be characterized as a weapon, Senator. Anesthesia. Radiation. Sound itself. We are not proposing that these frequencies be weaponized. We are proposing that they be understood."

"The Dual Use Research of Concern framework exists for exactly this reason."

"With respect, Senator, the DURC framework was designed for biological agents and synthetic pathogens. Acoustic resonance is a naturally occurring phenomenon. Every stone church in Europe produces frequencies in this range. Every Ashanti funeral drum produces frequencies in this range. The earth produces these frequencies continuously. Classifying geological sound as dual-use research of concern would require you to classify the planet itself as a potential weapon."

Holt removed her glasses. The gesture was performative. Holt's vision was fine, the glasses were reading glasses she wore for the papers she had already memorized. The removal was the signal that the official questioning was over and the unofficial assessment was beginning.

"Ms. Volkov. Off the record."

"Senator, there is no off the record in a hearing room."

"Then on the record: I am going to recommend that your Foundation's protocol be classified as DURC-adjacent pending full review by the NIH and the NSABB. This is not a judgment on your work. This is a judgment on the world your work exists in. You have demonstrated that acoustic frequencies can produce measurable neurological effects. You have also demonstrated, through no fault of your own, that those effects can be harmful when the preparation is omitted. I cannot control who reads your publications. I can control whether those publications carry a classification that requires oversight."

Nadia sat with this the way she sat with everything. The structural attention. The load-bearing wall. The institutional frequency that nobody measured.

"Senator, I understand. I want to say one thing for the record."

"Go ahead."

"The preparation is the protection. If you classify the frequency without classifying the preparation, you classify the fire without classifying the furnace. And the next person who builds a furnace will build it without knowing why the people inside it need fifty-two weeks before they walk in."

Holt put her glasses back on. Looked at Nadia over the frames, the look of a woman who agreed but could not say so, because agreement was not the function of a committee chair. Governance was.

"This hearing is adjourned. We will reconvene in sixty days with a full DURC review."

The gavel. The sound of wood on wood, a frequency Nadia could have measured if she'd had the equipment. She didn't need the equipment. She could hear it: the gavel's fundamental, the desk's response, the room's reverb. The institutional frequency of American governance: the sound of a system that was slow because it was large and large because it was trying, imperfectly, to contain everything.

She walked out of Dirksen 106 with the blazer buttoned and the briefing folder under her arm and the knowledge that the preparation was about to become the property of a committee that did not understand it and could not be trusted to protect it and would eventually, through the ponderous machinery of democratic oversight, do the right thing, not because the committee was wise but because the system was designed by people who understood that wisdom takes time, which is another way of saying the system was designed by people who understood preparation.

In the cab back to Reagan National, she called Blake.

"They're classifying it," she said. "DURC-adjacent. Sixty-day review."

A pause. Iceland breathing. "Is that bad?"

"It's institutional. Institutional is slow. Slow is good."

"Since when is slow good?"

"Since always, Blake. Slow is the preparation. Slow is the fifty-two weeks. I have been telling you this for seven years."

He laughed. The sound came through the phone with the faint compression of a satellite call, the audio slightly flattened, the frequencies above four thousand hertz removed by the bandwidth limitations, the laugh arriving in Kansas City as a slightly smaller version of itself, the way all institutional communications arrive slightly smaller than the thing they carry.

"I know," he said. "I'm a slow learner."

"You are an excellent learner. You are a slow listener."

"There's a difference?"

"There is always a difference."

She hung up. The cab passed the Lincoln Memorial, white stone, floodlit, the massive seated figure visible through the columns. A man who had been slow. A man who had taken four years to issue a document that should have been issued at the founding. A man who understood preparation.

Nadia looked at the memorial and thought: He would have understood the fifty-two weeks.
